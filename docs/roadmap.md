# Roadmap

What Homeostatic does today, what's being worked on next, and what's further out. Last updated October 17, 2026.

## What works now

- A unified policy list for supplied and user-created monitoring. Fresh setup enables integration, device and entity availability, Repairs and broken automations. Batteries and vacuums start off; temperature, thermostat and UPS templates need configuration. Supplied notification assignments start without delivery methods, and global sending is off.
- Availability checks derived from Home Assistant entity reports, with separate device evidence shown in Monitoring. The operational-member coverage change is tracked below.
- Battery policies with configurable percentage thresholds, initially 20%, plus reported low and charging evidence. Overlapping policies keep independent detection and notification settings.
- UPS checks for NUT and APC UPS Daemon: backup operation, low reserve, replacement warnings and faults, with grouped independent issues and reserve schedules for mains and backup mode.
- Vacuum errors. Home Assistant error activity opens an issue; cleaning, docked, idle, paused or returning ends it. The vacuum policy owns its notification entries.
- One issue per failure. When an integration fails, the devices and entities you watch on it are listed on that one issue.
- Home Assistant Repairs appear as issues, with notification entries in their policy.
- Automations that name an entity that no longer exists. The issue uses the automation's name, lists the missing entity ids, and opens the automation in the editor. Its policy owns the notification entries. An entity that is only unavailable stays out, and so does an entity id written only inside a template. Scripts are left out.
- Notifications to people's phones through the Companion app, with reusable named notifications and delivery methods, explicit test requests and taps that open the issue.
- The controls Acknowledge issue and Pause notifications. Active maintenance windows remain visible and can be ended; new windows use Home Assistant actions. Pauses and maintenance last up to seven days.
- History of ended issues: the last 100, from the past 30 days.
- Events for your own automations, a card for each panel page, and a ready-made dashboard.
- Optional grouping of Monitoring by TopoMation locations.
- Ordinary editable policies with a fixed monitoring type, capability-based selectors, independent duplicates and overlaps, enable/disable, deletion and selective restoration. Updates preserve edits and deletions. Each policy, named notification and delivery method retains its own draft across navigation and saves independently. Policies choose named notifications in their editor.
- Thermostat checks using configurable deviation, progress and worsening thresholds. Temperature-limit checks use chosen low/high bounds and an explicit duration. Both preserve missing-evidence and restart boundaries; overlapping temperature policies operate independently. Detection edits end the old monitoring expectation and restart qualification.
- Policies select reusable named notifications that own timing and delivery methods. Six editable defaults cover immediate delivery, acknowledgment reminders, daily hours and morning, evening or weekly summaries. Independent assignments remain supported. Shared edits affect saved uses; existing Custom notification assignments remain unchanged until explicitly replaced.
- App, voice, email and SMS requests through configured Home Assistant destinations, with fixed or presence-based selection and an explicit fallback or skip choice. Pending requests retain their resolved targets after restart, and cleanup uses prior destinations. Saved unchanged methods have one Send test action in their editor; there is no separate test area. Isolated tests cover the action mappings and presence behavior; provider-specific voice controls and live-provider trials remain follow-up work.

Supported unified policies and Custom notification assignments survive updates unchanged. The earlier pre-policy schedule-library configuration remains unsupported and is never automatically converted or reset. See [Upgrade and roll back](install.md#7-upgrade-and-roll-back).

The [Homeostatic manual](guide.md) covers supported setup and operation. What Homeostatic can't establish is listed under [limitations](manual/limitations.md). Automation-defined alerts remain unfinished and are outside the operating manual.

## Current work

The next policy addition is jammed locks, specified in [proposed ADR 0075](adr/0075-report-jammed-locks.md). The decision draft is ready for review; implementation and validation have not started. Completion requires the policy editor and state mapping, executable lifecycle and notification scenarios, and corresponding specification, guide and changelog updates.

The [shared device issue workflow](adr/0094-one-device-issue-workflow.md) is implemented, installed and verified for a local trial. Open issues and Monitoring open the same dialog for current issues independently owned by that device, including partial availability and other conditions, with individual response exceptions. The [acceptance and installation receipt](performance/device-issue-workflow-2026-10-17.json) records selection and ownership scenarios, rendered desktop and phone workflows, enlarged-text inspection, the flat Stop Monitoring confirmation and live verification with unchanged saved settings. The earlier availability-only build remains recorded in its [historical trial receipt](performance/device-availability-selection-2026-10-17.json). These UI changes are committed and remain unreleased. Physical Safari and full release acceptance remain outside this trial; other non-entity inspector simplification remains separate unfinished work.

Save and refresh latency on the household installation was measured and attributed on 2026-10-17 without code changes; [Save performance](save-performance.md#live-attribution-of-a-stop-monitoring-action) and the [live latency receipt](performance/live-latency-2026-10-17.json) hold the method and raw captures. A Stop Monitoring action takes 7 to 15 s from click to a settled panel. Each save and each 60 second reconcile tick runs a full discovery of about 5.5 s on the event loop, and about 85% of that discovery is Home Assistant deprecation-frame inspection from reading the deprecated `DeviceEntry.config_entries` property; the catalog reload after an edit adds 1.5 to 3 s. The property reads are replaced in the working tree: discovery reads each device's owning entry directly, phone routes use the registry's config-entry index, and executable scenarios fail if the deprecated properties are read again. The [after receipt](performance/live-latency-after-2026-10-17.json) and the [follow-up section](save-performance.md#after-replacing-the-deprecated-property-reads) record twelve actions on the same installation: the median save response fell from 7.3 to 3.2 s, the settled panel from 8.8 to 5.3 s, and the per-minute reconcile block from about 5 s to 0.8 s. This change is installed on the household installation and remains uncommitted and unreleased. The reconcile cadence, a catalog delta instead of a full reload, and persistence costs follow, each with a live before and after; the catalog reload is now the largest remaining share of a Stop Monitoring action.

Responsive refresh and saving under [ADR 0092](adr/0092-scoped-refresh-and-coherent-saves.md) is implemented and included in the local trial. Configuration reads remain coherent during saves; ordinary temperature evidence and policy renames avoid discovery; source indexes and bounded pickers avoid repeated work; detail reads coalesce; and the Issues list opens from its current context. Opening an availability issue loads the complete catalog needed by its guarded edit. The [acceptance and measurement receipt](performance/refresh-save-2026-10-17.json) preserves concurrency and delivery tests, desktop/phone workflows, the corrected issue-context failures, and the existing upstream Home Assistant reconnect failure. [Save performance](save-performance.md#scoped-refresh-and-saving-follow-up) records the measured reductions and remaining snapshot and observation-drain costs. Physical Safari, sustained household load and full accessibility remain outside this acceptance; a pre-existing shared-header overlap at 320 pixels with doubled text remains recorded.

The installed issue-dialog work provides visible evidence, named policy and notification editors with a return to the issue, and consistent Stop Monitoring actions under [ADR 0091](adr/0091-direct-issue-actions-and-coverage.md). This work is included in the local trial. The [bounded acceptance receipt](performance/issue-dialog-2026-10-17.json) records rendered desktop, phone and enlarged-text workflows, native Home Assistant navigation, exact availability-exclusion safeguards, visual inspection and required quick checks. Physical Safari, real delivery providers and representative household load remain outside this acceptance.

Version 1.8.3 keeps availability issues independent under healthy integrations under [ADR 0090](adr/0090-independent-issues-under-healthy-integrations.md), preserves controls when existing issues separate, and removes inactive grouping controls. The coordinated health-tree 0.8.0 and Homeostatic 1.8.3 releases are published. The [release receipt](performance/release-1.8.3.json) ties the tested desktop and phone paths to the committed files and published archive, with full CI, metadata and clean-install checks. The earlier [development receipt](performance/independent-issues-2026-10-16.json) retains intermediate attempts and their limits; the exact release candidate subsequently passed both source-only popup routes.

Entity inspectors use linked device and integration names, concise condition and coverage text, and one contextual Monitoring policies action. Opened issues show affected names and conditions directly and keep their complete action scope beside acknowledgment and notification pauses. Existing maintenance remains visible and can be ended. The decisions are recorded in [ADR 0085](adr/0085-device-centered-issue-context.md), [ADR 0086](adr/0086-visible-evidence-and-flat-details.md), [ADR 0087](adr/0087-remove-maintenance-creation-from-ui.md), [ADR 0088](adr/0088-show-information-that-supports-a-decision.md) and [ADR 0089](adr/0089-contextual-entity-monitoring-policies.md).

Policy source scope uses the visible Include radio choices published in [1.8.1](https://github.com/mjcumming/homeostatic/releases/tag/v1.8.1). Its [workflow and visual receipt](performance/policy-scope-radios-2026-10-15/receipt.json) retains that earlier acceptance scope.

Device availability policy editing now separates whole devices from ignored entity evidence, with ignored entities grouped under their device. Changing device scope retains the ignored members. This work is included in the local trial. The [acceptance receipt](performance/device-exclusions-2026-10-16.json) records passing desktop/phone rendered scenarios, quick checks and focused Python tests. The later [grouping verification](performance/independent-issues-2026-10-16.json) also records a passing real-backend device-exclusion scenario using the working Windows browser with isolated Linux Home Assistant. Physical Safari remains untested. This implements the existing exclusion decision without a new ADR or migration.

This section owns implementation and acceptance status. The integration specification owns current behavior; design documents and ADRs retain requirements and rationale. Evidence links identify the relevant checks, without claiming that the full suite or live acceptance has passed for the current working tree.

## Next

### Other planned work

- Qualify save performance under sustained household load. The bounded method workflow now passes without runtime reload, with six server transactions between 0.635 and 1.465 seconds. The first browser observation still included an unattributed delay before the measured handler; checkpoint variation, pending observations and periodic reconciliation also need broader qualification. The [performance record](save-performance.md) separates isolated measurements, live server timings and actual-browser observations.
- Complete automation-defined alerts before presenting them as ready for user setup. Existing experimental reporting interfaces do not constitute a finished product workflow. Define how alert notification choices join the current policy workflow, preserve saved reporting profiles and the public alert service, and add a supported setup path for new outgoing alerts. The removed fixed-reporting editor must not return as an alternate configuration system.
- Bound large issue event payloads so Home Assistant Recorder can retain them. The local pilot reports that some `homeostatic_episode` events exceed its 32,768-byte limit. The warning occurred before and after the UI trial; resolve it without silently dropping the evidence exposed by issue queries.
- Backup failures and overdue automatic backups. Use Home Assistant's [backup events and timestamps](https://www.home-assistant.io/integrations/backup/) to report a failed attempt with its reason, or a successful backup that is overdue for the owner's chosen interval. Link to Backups and define what evidence ends each issue. Discover the relevant entities rather than assuming their names.
- Health of the machine running Home Assistant. Cover low disk space, sustained CPU load, memory and swap pressure, I/O stalls, and high processor temperature using available [System Monitor](https://www.home-assistant.io/integrations/systemmonitor/) entities. Include reported power-supply or undervoltage problems where supported, such as the [Raspberry Pi power checker](https://www.home-assistant.io/integrations/rpi_power/). Prefer pressure measurements that show work waiting for resources where available. Identify whether a measurement describes the host, container or virtual machine, and use thresholds appropriate to that source and hardware. Require sustained evidence and a defined recovery condition; missing measurements remain missing evidence. Each issue should show the measurement and a useful next step.
- Recurring Home Assistant runtime errors that don't become Repairs. Explore selected integration polling and automation execution errors through [System Log events](https://www.home-assistant.io/integrations/system_log/). Group repeated occurrences, retain their count and latest evidence, and start with actionable patterns and summary reporting. Define recovery before implementation; a quiet log alone doesn't prove that the problem ended.
- Battery checks tested against real devices. The checks are released. The next step is to compare them with real battery and charging reports and refine what a battery issue tells you to do.
- A view for household members who aren't administrators. The panel is administrator-only today, and the permissions design comes before any change.

## Later

Evaluate additions against the [product scope](product-specification.md#product-scope): prioritize Home Assistant and equipment problems, and require a common household need for other built-in policy types. Specialized conditions belong in Home Assistant automations.

- Standard reported conditions have separate proposed decisions: [problem sensors](adr/0076-report-problem-sensors.md), [lawn mower errors](adr/0077-report-lawn-mower-errors.md), [tamper](adr/0078-report-tamper-sensors.md), [unsafe conditions](adr/0079-report-safety-sensors.md), [water leaks](adr/0080-report-moisture-sensors.md), [smoke](adr/0081-report-smoke-sensors.md), [gas](adr/0082-report-gas-sensors.md), [carbon monoxide](adr/0083-report-carbon-monoxide-sensors.md) and [triggered alarms](adr/0084-report-triggered-alarms.md). Each identifies its audience, useful response, selection requirements and recovery evidence. Implementation has not started. Problem sensors follow jammed locks in priority; the other proposals have no committed implementation order. Completion requires the policy editor and observation mapping, executable lifecycle and mocked notification scenarios, and specification, guide and changelog updates. Proposed notification defaults remain subject to review.
- Device availability reported by integrations themselves. Home Assistant has a proposal for integrations to report device availability directly. Homeostatic already follows that model and will read those reports once Home Assistant ships them.
- Consider checks for stale readings or stalled detectors only where a common monitoring need and reliable evidence justify a built-in policy. Each proposal needs recordings from real devices of normal running, failure and recovery. Individual command-completion checks, including whether a cover reached a commanded position, stay outside the built-in scope and belong in Home Assistant automations.
- A watchdog that runs outside Home Assistant, so you hear about it when Home Assistant itself stops.
- Watching every entity on very large installs, 6,000 or more. This needs testing under real household load on the hardware people actually run.
- Reconsidering household functions if a clear use case and a useful setup workflow emerge. Their stored definitions are dormant.
- A Snooze button on phone notifications.
- More ways to set up alerts: a guided menu of common alerts in Homeostatic, and alerts for one-off events that have no start and end. The condition itself stays in a Home Assistant automation.
- More from TopoMation, such as occupancy and automation context.
- A full history that records every setting change, control and notification, beyond the ended issues History keeps now.
- Suggestions for tidying up settings when you've built up many single-entity rules.

These are ideas under consideration, with no commitment yet:

- Consumables and scheduled maintenance, starting with one kind that has reliable data.
- Grouping maintenance into tasks with a list of supplies, once quantities can be trusted.
- Showing maintenance in Home Assistant's own screens, if Home Assistant offers a supported way to do it.
- Problems reported by other tools, where they say clearly when a problem starts and ends.
- Looking up supplies information, like which battery a device takes, from an outside source.

Maintainers: the reasons behind current behavior and these plans are in the [decision records](adr/README.md).
