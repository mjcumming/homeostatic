# Roadmap

What Homeostatic does today, what's being worked on next, and what's further out. Last updated October 24, 2026.

## What works now

- A unified policy list for supplied and user-created monitoring. Fresh setup enables the supplied policies, including Batteries and Vacuums. Newly supplied policies enter existing installations once, disabled; existing edits, enabled states and deletions stay unchanged. Temperature, thermostat and UPS templates need configuration. Supplied notification assignments start without delivery methods, and global sending is off.
- Availability checks follow Home Assistant entity reports, with related entity readings grouped by device in Monitoring. The operational-member coverage change is tracked below.
- Battery policies with configurable percentage thresholds, initially 20%, plus reported low and charging evidence. Overlapping policies keep independent detection and notification settings.
- UPS checks for NUT and APC UPS Daemon: backup operation, low reserve, replacement warnings and faults, with grouped independent issues and reserve schedules for mains and backup mode.
- Vacuum errors. Home Assistant error activity opens an issue; cleaning, docked, idle, paused or returning ends it. The vacuum policy owns its notification entries.
- One issue per failure. When an integration fails, the devices and entities you watch on it are listed on that one issue.
- Home Assistant Repairs appear as issues, with notification entries in their policy.
- Automations that name an entity that no longer exists. The issue uses the automation's name, lists the missing entity ids, and opens the automation in the editor. Its policy owns the notification entries. An entity that is only unavailable stays out, and so does an entity id written only inside a template. Scripts are left out.
- Notifications to people's phones through the Companion app, with reusable named notifications and delivery methods, explicit test requests and taps that open the issue.
- The controls Acknowledge issue and Pause notifications. Active maintenance windows remain visible and can be ended; new windows use Home Assistant actions. Pauses and maintenance last up to seven days.
- History of ended issues: the last 100, from the past 30 days.
- Events for your own automations, a card for each panel page, and a ready-made dashboard.
- Optional grouping of Monitoring by TopoMation locations.
- Ordinary editable policies with a fixed monitoring type, capability-based selectors, independent duplicates and overlaps, enable/disable, deletion and selective restoration. Updates preserve edits and deletions. Each policy, named notification and delivery method retains its own draft across navigation and saves independently. Policies choose named notifications in their editor.
- Thermostat checks using configurable deviation, progress and worsening thresholds. Temperature-limit checks use chosen low/high bounds and an explicit duration. Both preserve missing-evidence and restart boundaries; overlapping temperature policies operate independently. Detection edits end the old monitoring expectation and restart qualification.
- Policies select reusable named notifications that own timing and delivery methods. Six editable defaults cover immediate delivery, acknowledgment reminders, daily hours and morning, evening or weekly summaries. Independent assignments remain supported. Shared edits affect saved uses; existing Custom notification assignments remain unchanged until explicitly replaced.
- App, voice, email and SMS requests through configured Home Assistant destinations, with fixed or presence-based selection and an explicit fallback or skip choice. Pending requests retain their resolved targets after restart, and cleanup uses prior destinations. Saved unchanged methods have one Send test action in their editor; there is no separate test area. Isolated tests cover the action mappings and presence behavior; provider-specific voice controls and live-provider trials remain follow-up work.

Homeostatic preserves supported earlier notification settings by adding zero collection delay. Existing pre-collection delivery state stops setup before settings changes or message requests; it must remain paired with its matching prior installation or backup. Current-format policies and Custom notification assignments survive ordinary saves and restarts unchanged. Unsupported configuration is never silently reset. See [Upgrade and roll back](install.md#7-upgrade-and-roll-back).

The [Homeostatic manual](guide.md) covers supported setup and operation. What Homeostatic can't establish is listed under [limitations](manual/limitations.md). Automation-defined alerts remain unfinished and are outside the operating manual.

Save and refresh latency on the household installation was measured and attributed on 2026-10-17 and 2026-10-18; [Save performance](save-performance.md#live-attribution-of-a-stop-monitoring-action) holds the method and the receipts under docs/performance retain the summarized measurements. The deprecated device property reads were replaced (median save response 7.3 s to 3.2 s, per-minute reconcile block 5 s to 0.8 s), and [ADR 0095](adr/0095-patch-catalog-after-edits.md) is implemented, installed and measured: the panel requests no catalog pages after an edit and the median settled time fell from 5.3 s to 3.2 s (see the [catalog delta receipt](performance/live-latency-catalog-delta-2026-10-17.json)). A permanent debug-level trace then [attributed the remaining second](save-performance.md#what-the-remaining-second-is): the enrollment report after a rule change (0.81 s of a save), generation-2 garbage collections of about 0.85 s that explain every pause over half a second, and discovery (0.46 s per save and per minute). Reusing unchanged candidate rows in the enrollment report, and then reusing the registry scan and unchanged decisions for edits that change only exclusions, are implemented, installed and measured: the settled panel after a Stop fell from 3.35 s to 2.38 s and then to 1.52 s, with the server save at 1.33 s (see the [candidate rows receipt](performance/live-latency-candidate-rows-2026-10-17.json) and the [scan reuse receipt](performance/live-latency-scan-reuse-2026-10-17.json)). [ADR 0096](adr/0096-coalesce-evidence-saves-and-verify-bytes.md) is implemented, installed and measured: saves verify by comparing stored bytes and evidence-only refreshes coalesce, which cut idle saves from one per refresh to 0.85 per refresh, idle generation-2 collections from 0.4 to 0.27 a minute and the storage phase of a save from 0.20 s to 0.11 s, while the Stop medians did not improve because a collection still lands inside about one edit in two (see the [coalesced saves receipt](performance/live-latency-coalesced-saves-2026-10-17.json)). The next outcome is footprint rather than latency: the installation rewrites a 7.7 MB file about 1.5 times a minute (about 17 GB a day) because the minute reconcile stamps observation times into persisted evidence, and 1.4 MB of the file is route signatures that store rule content instead of a digest (see the [footprint measurement](performance/snapshot-footprint-2026-10-18.json)). The [core design audit](core-design-audit-2026-10-18.md) defines the minimal core and its order of work. Step 1, [ADR 0097](adr/0097-shrink-the-snapshot-and-save-durable-changes.md), is implemented for the adapter and measured: route signatures persist as digests and evidence as the last known failure, which cut the adapter's persisted sections from 1.83 MB to 0.25 MB of compact JSON, while writes per idle minute stayed at the engine's cadence because the engine's check records change every minute (see the [durable facts receipt](performance/durable-facts-2026-10-18.json)). Step 3, [ADR 0098](adr/0098-update-the-subject-and-sweep-rarely.md), is implemented, installed and measured: integration entries are observed only when discovering or at a retry deadline, a config-entry update that changes nothing visible requests no refresh, and the minute reconciliation is a 15-minute sweep; idle writes fell from 1.75 to 0.71 a minute and a quiet minute no longer changes the file (see the [sweep receipt](performance/sweep-2026-10-18.json)). Step 2, one map of each source's latest observation, is implemented. Step 4 shipped as health-tree 0.9.0, whose snapshots hold durable state only; with that library installed, the store file fell from 7.2 MB to 6.7 MB, and two copies a minute apart are byte-identical, so writes now follow real changes (see the [library snapshots receipt](performance/library-snapshots-2026-10-18.json)). Step 5, [ADR 0100](adr/0100-a-failed-settings-save-reloads-from-the-store.md), removes the settings-save checkpoint: a failed save reports the error and reloads from the store. The audit's order of work is complete. Open afterwards: a trace capture with a signed-in session that attributes the remaining saves by engine event type, the policy snapshot's per-route copies of every tracked episode (1.6 MB, a health-tree size question), and the household conditions the measurements surfaced, a source that opens and resolves an issue every few minutes and the stale issues left by the 13:37Z restart.

Define how alert notification choices join the current policy workflow, preserve saved reporting profiles and the public alert service, and add a supported setup path for new outgoing alerts. The removed fixed-reporting editor must not return as an alternate configuration system.
- Consider a versioned event contract if extremely large machine fields need guaranteed Recorder retention. The current preview and short-link fix preserves complete schema-one membership; its 1,000-member probe still exceeds Recorder's limit. Detected-fact findings remain complete too. The [verification](performance/recorder-event-size-2026-10-23.json), [research](performance/notification-payload-research-2026-10-23.json) and [fix receipt](performance/notification-payload-fix-2026-10-23.json) distinguish the verified notification correction from this compatibility tradeoff. The earlier provider failure's cause remains unproven.

The existing `updates.retention` browser case retains the draft value but fails its same-input DOM assertion on both the notification candidate and an unchanged `main` build. The [notification fix receipt](performance/notification-payload-fix-2026-10-23.json) links the baseline comparison. This separate issue was not changed by the notification fix.
- Updates policy type with a supplied policy named Updates. Report available updates through the existing weekly notification workflow, with each issue linking to the corresponding update in Home Assistant. Homeostatic reports the available update; the owner reviews and performs the work in Home Assistant. Keep this policy scoped to updates, with backup monitoring separate.
- Backup failures and overdue automatic backups, monitored through a separate policy type from Updates. Use Home Assistant's [backup events and timestamps](https://www.home-assistant.io/integrations/backup/) to report a failed attempt with its reason, or a successful backup that is overdue for the owner's chosen interval. Link to Backups and define what evidence ends each issue. Discover the relevant entities rather than assuming their names.
- Health of the machine running Home Assistant. Cover low disk space, sustained CPU load, memory and swap pressure, I/O stalls, and high processor temperature using available [System Monitor](https://www.home-assistant.io/integrations/systemmonitor/) entities. Include reported power-supply or undervoltage problems where supported, such as the [Raspberry Pi power checker](https://www.home-assistant.io/integrations/rpi_power/). Prefer pressure measurements that show work waiting for resources where available. Identify whether a measurement describes the host, container or virtual machine, and use thresholds appropriate to that source and hardware. Require sustained evidence and a defined recovery condition; missing measurements remain missing evidence. Each issue should show the measurement and a useful next step.
- Recurring Home Assistant runtime errors that don't become Repairs. Explore selected integration polling and automation execution errors through [System Log events](https://www.home-assistant.io/integrations/system_log/). Group repeated occurrences, retain their count and latest evidence, and start with actionable patterns and summary reporting. Define recovery before implementation; a quiet log alone doesn't prove that the problem ended.

## Later

Evaluate additions against the [product scope](spec.md#product-scope): prioritize Home Assistant and equipment problems, and require a common household need for other built-in policy types. Specialized conditions belong in Home Assistant automations.

- Standard reported conditions have separate proposed decisions: [problem sensors](adr/0076-report-problem-sensors.md), [lawn mower errors](adr/0077-report-lawn-mower-errors.md), [tamper](adr/0078-report-tamper-sensors.md), [unsafe conditions](adr/0079-report-safety-sensors.md), [water leaks](adr/0080-report-moisture-sensors.md), [smoke](adr/0081-report-smoke-sensors.md), [gas](adr/0082-report-gas-sensors.md), [carbon monoxide](adr/0083-report-carbon-monoxide-sensors.md) and [triggered alarms](adr/0084-report-triggered-alarms.md). Each identifies its audience, useful response, selection requirements and recovery evidence. Implementation has not started. Problem sensors follow jammed locks in priority; the other proposals have no committed implementation order. Completion requires the policy editor and observation mapping, executable lifecycle and mocked notification scenarios, and specification, guide and changelog updates. Proposed notification defaults remain subject to review.
- Device availability reported by integrations themselves. Home Assistant has a proposal for integrations to report device availability directly. Homeostatic already follows that model and will read those reports once Home Assistant ships them.
- Consider checks for stale readings or stalled detectors only where a common monitoring need and reliable evidence justify a built-in policy. Each proposal needs recordings from real devices of normal running, failure and recovery. Individual command-completion checks, including whether a cover reached a commanded position, stay outside the built-in scope and belong in Home Assistant automations.
- A watchdog that runs outside Home Assistant, so you hear about it when Home Assistant itself stops.
- Watching every entity on very large installs, 6,000 or more. This needs testing under real household load on the hardware people actually run.
- Reconsidering household functions if a clear use case and a useful setup workflow emerge. Their stored definitions are dormant.
- A Snooze button on phone notifications.
- More ways to set up alerts: a guided menu of common alerts in Homeostatic, and alerts for one-off events that have no start and end. The condition itself stays in a Home Assistant automation.
- More from TopoMation, such as occupancy and automation context.
- A full history that records every setting change, control and notification, beyond the ended issues History keeps now.
- Suggestions for tidying up settings when you've built up many single-entity rules.

These are ideas under consideration, with no commitment yet:

- Consumables and scheduled maintenance, starting with one kind that has reliable data.
- Grouping maintenance into tasks with a list of supplies, once quantities can be trusted.
- Showing maintenance in Home Assistant's own screens, if Home Assistant offers a supported way to do it.
- Problems reported by other tools, where they say clearly when a problem starts and ends.
- Looking up supplies information, like which battery a device takes, from an outside source.

Maintainers: the reasons behind current behavior and these plans are in the [decision records](adr/README.md).
