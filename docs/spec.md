# Homeostatic specification

Date: 2026-10-18. Development baseline: Home Assistant 2026.9.3 and Python 3.14.

This document is the contract for Homeostatic: what the product is for, how its interface behaves and what the integration does. It owns current behavior. The [accepted decisions](adr/README.md) explain why; the [roadmap](roadmap.md) owns implementation status and unfinished scope; [events.md](events.md) owns event payloads. The library design of record is `health-tree/docs/rfp.md`, and its accepted ADRs govern engine and policy behavior. HealthTree owns all attention decisions, including activation and reminder holds.

## Product purpose and scope

Homeostatic helps an owner run Home Assistant. It identifies problems in monitored integrations, devices, entities and reported situations, and requests notifications according to the owner's policies. Someone using it should be able to understand a problem, act on it and predict what will happen with notifications without reconstructing the system's internal structure.

The interface serves the administrator who configures monitoring and investigates issues, and the panel is administrator-only. Notification recipients need understandable messages and useful destinations within their existing access. Access for household members who aren't administrators is separate planned work. These are design roles rather than findings from a usability study.

### Product scope

Homeostatic's core scope is operating Home Assistant and its connected equipment: integration, device and entity availability, reported faults and errors, Repairs, battery problems and broken configuration. Checks should help someone recognize a problem and decide what to do about it.

Additional built-in policy types must address common household monitoring needs with a reusable condition, understandable settings and credible evidence of both the problem and its recovery. Thermostat progress and temperature limits meet this test: inadequate heating or cooling, freeze risk and refrigeration warming are common concerns, and the checks assess reported temperatures against configured expectations without waiting for Home Assistant to report an error.

Review a proposed policy by asking who needs to know, why the condition matters and what useful action they could take. Identify the common use across households, the evidence available and the setup burden. A condition being technically detectable is insufficient reason to add a policy type.

Specialized conditions, household-specific sequences and individual command-completion checks belong in Home Assistant automations. Verifying that a cover reached a commanded position is outside the built-in policy scope, while a fault explicitly reported by that equipment remains a candidate for core monitoring. Automation authors own custom detection and recovery logic; the unfinished workflow for bringing their alerts into Homeostatic is tracked in the [roadmap](roadmap.md#other-planned-work).

Equipment repair and reauthentication remain in Home Assistant. Homeostatic never retries, enables, re-authenticates or commands equipment on its own. A reported Home Assistant state is evidence about Home Assistant's control path: it does not prove that a device is physically fresh, that a command completed or that a reading is correct, and no adapter timer is a device heartbeat. Device freshness, Frigate liveness, command completion, update catalogs and external probes would each need their own producer contract and real traces. Missing, unknown, unavailable, restored and failed-to-retrieve evidence stay distinct throughout this contract, and none of them establishes recovery. Delivery means a request was made, never that a message was received or read.

### User goals

| Goal | Successful outcome |
| --- | --- |
| Find what needs attention | Identify a concrete condition and its affected entities, devices, automations or other named sources from Open issues or a notification. |
| Understand the evidence | Distinguish the current Home Assistant report, the monitoring expectation and the issue that expectation created. |
| Understand coverage | Identify each check on a source, why it applies and the policy to change. |
| Understand notifications | Distinguish saved configuration from the evaluated notification behavior for an open issue. |
| Make a deliberate change | Predict its scope, save it once and see a confirmed result or a useful error. |
| Reduce unwanted attention | Adjust monitoring, timing, destinations or a temporary pause while understanding what continues. |
| Investigate earlier problems | Read retained outcomes without treating missing history as proof that nothing happened. |
| Keep your place | Move between an issue, source, policy and method, then return with context and drafts intact. |

These observable outcomes guide review. Every heading, control, section, disclosure and navigation step must help someone understand useful information, make a decision or take an action. [Purpose before controls](#purpose-before-controls) is the test, applied before an element is added and again during rendered review.

### Concepts and ownership

| Concept | Meaning in the interface | Owner |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Source | An identifiable integration, device, entity, automation or reported situation being inspected. | Home Assistant owns equipment identity and configuration; Homeostatic shows evidence and monitoring context. |
| Check | One monitoring assessment applied to a source. Several checks may use the same source. | Its policy, or the owning Home Assistant automation for automation-defined situations. |
| Issue | An engine-established problem with a stable identity, evidence, recorded members and lifecycle. | Issue details expose actions on that exact issue. |
| Investigation group | Related issues presented together to help locate and investigate problems. | View state; expanding or filtering it performs no monitoring or issue action. |
| Policy | A named definition of what to watch, what constitutes a problem and its notification assignments. | One policy editor and draft. |
| Notification | Reusable name, timing and delivery-method choices for eligible problems. | Notifications owns the shared definition; each policy owns its assignments. |
| Delivery method | A reusable destination choice, optionally including presence behavior. | Its shared editor and explicit save scope. |
| Global notification sending | Whether ordinary issue notification requests are enabled for the installation. | An independently applied operational control available throughout the interface. |

One actual source has one navigational identity. Its independent checks and policies appear inside its details, and a second check must not produce a second apparent light or sensor. Policies may intentionally overlap; the interface exposes each contribution without silent merging or replacement. [ADR 0064](adr/0064-monitoring-coverage-and-device-evidence.md) owns monitoring eligibility and notification consolidation.

## Scope and setup

One Homeostatic config entry per installation. Home Assistant creates it in one step; there is no Configure flow, native options form or integration YAML. Policies is the monitoring editor from fresh setup onward. Fresh setup installs the supplied policies and notifications listed under [Supplied policies and defaults](#supplied-policies-and-defaults), with global notification sending off.

The catalog uses Home Assistant config-entry state and entity availability evidence only. Registered entities use registry identity; state-only entities use the explicitly weaker `entity_id:` identity. Homeostatic's own entities and config entries are ineligible. Monitoring summaries distinguish integration instances, device summaries, entities with devices and entities without devices; these are inventory groupings rather than inferred physical or software classifications.

Disabled sources, including integrations and add-ons, remain accessible in Monitoring without availability issues solely due to disablement. Monitoring hides disabled sources by default in every grouping and search view; Show disabled sources reveals them, and their details explain: "Monitoring paused while disabled. Saved monitoring choices resume when re-enabled." The toggle changes browsing only. Config entries with `disabled_by` set receive no availability checks from catalog rules or named policies. Disabling an enrolled entry removes its check through the engine's existing removal lifecycle without producing passing evidence or claiming recovery, and saved monitoring choices apply again on re-enablement. Related sources and Repairs retain their independent eligibility. Homeostatic has no separate Supervisor add-on inventory or runtime check; add-on-related sources follow their registry eligibility. A stopped add-on, disabled automatic startup, an unloaded integration or missing evidence does not establish disablement, and any future native add-on producer must use an explicit disablement signal.

### Conversion helpers and groups

`switch_as_x` (Change device type of a switch) config entries are ineligible. They wrap an existing switch to expose it as another entity type, so counting the helper entry as another monitored device would duplicate the underlying capability. The converted light, fan or other entity remains eligible for its own availability check, without a dependency on the omitted helper entry. The exclusion applies by source type, including in installations whose saved policies match every integration ([ADR 0001](adr/0001-omit-switch-conversion-helper-entries.md)).

`group` config entries and their group entities remain eligible because an owner can use a group as an aggregate control target in automations. The config-entry check reports whether Home Assistant loaded the helper, and the entity check reports whether it currently exposes the aggregate entity. The Group row counts those sources rather than the devices inside the group, and is neither a physical device nor a member-health rollup. In the pinned Home Assistant implementation a light or binary-sensor group can remain available while a member is unavailable, so a passing group check never clears, replaces or mutes an individual member's problem; monitor member entities separately when their operation matters. Homeostatic creates no dependency edges and infers no device health from group membership. Keeping Group sources is a [proposed decision](adr/0002-retain-group-sources-as-limited-aggregate-evidence.md) pending review of their place and explanation in the list.

### Catalog selection

Named monitoring policies are the only equipment enrollment model. Policies compile their enabled selectors into internal catalog matching records and create independent condition identities. Catalog discovery is passive; disabled records remain browsable but ineligible for availability checks. Situations remain independent of equipment enrollment. There is no separate saved catalog-rule editor, inheritance hierarchy or rule-preview service.

Selectors match registry identities and metadata. Fields are ANDed and values within a field are ORed. Supported metadata includes kind, domain, device class, entity category, integration entry and family, device, entity, area, floor and labels. Entity area overrides device area; floor follows the effective area. Labels combine entity, device and effective-area labels. Integration sources have no inferred location membership. Names never drive matching. The public policy fields and selector modes are under [Selectors and source selection](#selectors-and-source-selection).

An entity policy can additionally save `match_any` or `match_none`, mutually exclusive nonempty lists of entity-type clauses. Each clause requires `domain` and can narrow `device_class`. The base selector still applies; `match_any` requires one matching clause and `match_none` excludes every matching clause. General entity availability starts with the complement of cameras, locks, alarm panels and binary sensors of class smoke, carbon_monoxide, gas or moisture; Important equipment availability starts with that union. Both apply existing enabled operational eligibility, including standalone entities. These independent saved expressions track future entities and metadata changes; neither queries the other policy's current choices, existence or enabled state, and editing one leaves the other unchanged. General uses Morning and Important equipment uses Immediate, with identical evidence, severity and holds. Ordinary editing can create overlaps or gaps without a winner or fallback ([ADR 0099](adr/0099-two-supplied-entity-availability-policies.md)).

New matching sources enroll on registry changes, state appearance or the 15-minute sweep. Metadata changes re-evaluate policy scope. Integration lifecycle evidence arrives through each watched entry's state callback; an entry update that changes neither title, domain nor disabled state requests no refresh, while entry additions, removals, renames, disablement and other catalog changes still rediscover. Inventory includes the discovered candidates and contributing policies, including unmonitored candidates. It retains at most 50 activity records for the current runtime: an initial scope snapshot, followed by changes grouped by reconciliation id. Activity is not persisted across reloads; it is diagnostic data and is not shown on History.

Ordinary temperature readings and thermostat target or action changes enter the ordered observation queue without invalidating the catalog. A selected thermostat changing between supported and unsupported operating modes still reconciles its checks. Missing, restored, unknown and unavailable evidence retains the existing enrollment rules. Unselected climate updates do not trigger discovery solely because a thermostat policy exists; relevant metadata changes and periodic reconciliation still apply.

### Entity availability checks

Availability policies monitor Home Assistant integrations or entities. Device registry records organize related entities and issues; they have no Homeostatic availability verdict, check, observation or inferred dependency, because Home Assistant does not expose generic device availability in its supported Core API. No speculative device-report reducer or ingestion path is retained.

An entity availability check declares that the entity is expected to remain available. Broad scopes include enabled operational entities, including entities without devices. Diagnostic and configuration entities require exact selection or an explicit inclusion. Disabled entities are never monitored. A current `unavailable` state is warning evidence. `unknown` is a provided state without a value and does not open an availability issue. A current `off` remains an ordinary state, including on connectivity sensors. Missing or restored states are absent evidence and follow the existing unknown hold; they cannot establish recovery.

Each selected entity has an independent policy-owned check and retains applicable integration dependencies. A sibling's current state cannot clear its issue. Removing an entity from a policy ends its check as monitoring removed; it does not declare recovery or affect another policy. Disabled or empty devices remain browsable. Device details show current entity readings, missing and restored evidence counts and the policies actually watching their entities, without assigning a device status.

On setup, saved device availability policies are converted under [ADR 0103](adr/0103-entity-availability-with-grouped-selection.md). Conversion retains identities, names, enabled states, notifications, exact device selections and exclusions. Unrestricted device scopes and device metadata scopes resolve to matching registry device identities once, retaining their future entity members without following later device area or label changes. New devices require selection, and standalone entities do not silently enter migrated device coverage. Unrestricted device policies become broad entity policies. The full candidate validates before storage changes, and conversion is idempotent. Reconciliation removes old device checks and ends their issues as removed; retained history is not rewritten. Published schema-two entity identity bindings remain supported so unrelated acknowledged entity issues survive that earlier storage upgrade.

The source query returns operational entity readings and complete counts for device organization. It no longer returns `device_availability`, and node replies no longer return the retired `device_evidence`. Inspected entity readings refresh from current Home Assistant states without synthesizing a device assessment.

### Retained identities and evidence meanings

Previously enrolled identities and their last match attributes are persisted independently of the opaque engine snapshot. A registered entity with missing state remains monitored with unknown evidence. An exact entity selection in a named policy or a retained function requirement keeps its entity expectation after registry deletion; an exact device selection keeps a saved device choice without an availability check until eligible members exist. Explicitly required config entries likewise remain unknown when absent, and a config entry that remains registered but is not loaded is unknown. When Home Assistant removes an automatically selected registry entity, the last eligible member of an automatically selected device summary, or an automatically selected config entry, Homeostatic removes that source, resolves any active episode as `removed` and retains the resolution in history. The removal event does not identify user intent and never establishes recovery. Ordinary live metadata replaces retained metadata when a selected identity is present again. A state-derived device class is retained while state evidence is missing, unknown, restored or unavailable. State-only identities are retained while state evidence is absent, but do not promise continuity across entity-id changes.

The first catalog checks config-entry state and entity availability. Disabled, temporarily not loaded, explicitly required but missing, startup or restored sources are unknown evidence. An entity state of `unknown` passes the passive availability check; it is neither an issue nor stale evidence by itself. A loaded config entry passes; setup retry warns for a configurable retry hold, then fails; setup and migration errors and failed unload fail; pending reauthentication fails with `auth_required`. Confirmed deletion of an automatically selected registry source is a scope removal rather than unknown evidence. An entity reporting `unavailable` warns. The producers under [Built-in checks](#built-in-checks) have their own bounded contracts.

## Time and lifecycle

Create or restore the engine only after Home Assistant has started. Load listeners and entities earlier, reporting unavailable until startup completes. One adapter owns calls into engine and policy on the HA event loop. Observations arriving together are ingested atomically. State and entry callbacks capture observations with their receipt time before queuing work. Every captured transition is applied in order, even if storage is busy. Each transition is its own observation batch; reconciliation must not erase it. Registry changes trigger an inventory refresh; a sweep every 15 minutes catches missed lifecycle changes, pending reauthentication and metadata retained for vanished sources, while the deadline timer covers the startup grace, the longest startup wait and each retry hold, re-observing an entry whose retry hold elapsed ([ADR 0098](adr/0098-update-the-subject-and-sweep-rarely.md)). A deadline timer uses the earlier engine/policy deadline, independently of reconciliation. No delay is added to batch unrelated observations. One queued refresh worker drains captured transitions in receipt order; redundant wakeups share that worker. New observations during persistence request another pass. A failed pass is retried by subsequent evidence or reconciliation, without a busy retry loop. Shutdown drains captured evidence under the same serialization lock and cannot rearm work.

Queued observations retain their timestamps, ordering and atomic batch boundaries. The adapter yields to the HA loop after eight captured batches or 20 ms of elapsed processing, whichever comes first, while retaining its runtime lock. Elapsed processing uses a monotonic clock, independently of observation UTC timestamps. The budget is checked after each complete batch and its policy work; it cannot interrupt one synchronous engine call. New arrivals join the queue and are drained before current-state reconciliation, controls, persistence and publication. The current time is read after draining, so time cannot move backward when new evidence arrives during a yield. No transition is dropped or collapsed by this scheduling. Graph evaluation, reconciliation, serialization and catalog transfer still require measurement.

Availability-only events use an entity-to-source index and the existing catalog. Entity registry changes, config-entry additions and removals, state arrival and removal, source-name and device-class changes and the explicit or 15-minute sweep rediscover inventory. A monitoring edit that changes only rules or exclusions re-evaluates the last registry scan without reading the registries again when nothing has changed since that scan, and keeps the decisions of candidates the edit does not name; an edit that selects different sources scans again, and the next reconciliation refreshes metadata retained for sources that have since disappeared. Periodic reconciliation still captures missed config-entry and reauthentication changes. An observation callback does not rebuild unchanged inventory or reread every entity. Deadlines reobserve enrolled integration conditions to preserve retry escalation and advance engine and policy timing. Public inventory queries remain detached from the cached metadata. Evidence updates and read-only queries reuse the compiled notification policy; source changes rebuild it, and a settings reload creates a new runtime. Unchanged reconciliation retains the existing compiled policy and its attention clocks. [ADR 0010](adr/0010-large-inventory-updates.md) records the ordered runtime and compact-transport decision; the catalog revision it introduced is described under [Live updates and subscriptions](#live-updates-and-subscriptions).

A sweep reads all enrolled sources into one observation batch; other refreshes observe only the sources whose events arrived. It registers new or changed nodes together through `Engine.register_many` before ingesting observations, preserving existing check state and validating the final topology. Initial setup likewise registers its graph in one batch; unchanged reconciliation does not register nodes again. It keeps missing selected nodes as unknown. Explicit unenrollment removes their nodes, and a removal notice is never presented as physical recovery.

State events select each check independently. Ordinary available-value changes do not trigger a new availability observation; changes between available, unavailable, unknown, missing and restored evidence do. Entities not provided observes only transitions between live states and missing or restored states, regardless of the live value. Its owner lifecycle and registry changes still refresh the prerequisites. Value-based checks, including vacuum activity, continue receiving readings even when those presence or availability conditions stay the same. When no selected check needs an event and no relevant metadata or consumer state changes, the callback queues no refresh, engine or notification-policy evaluation, publication or snapshot work. The periodic sweep still refreshes the available HA evidence.

Initial settings: settle 120 seconds, rejoin grace 60 seconds, startup grace 120 seconds, longest startup wait for integrations 600 seconds, coalescing count 3 and window 60 seconds, notification batch 30 seconds, clear hold 120 seconds, unknown hold 900 seconds. Availability failures have zero raise hold; setup retry has a 120-second warning period across unsuccessful setup attempts. SETUP_IN_PROGRESS does not reset the retry onset; leaving the retry cycle does. Installation Settings edits these durations and exposes active timing controls only. Availability checks use no observation expiry because they read HA's current state. Apply startup grace when Home Assistant starts; a Homeostatic reload while HA is running, including a panel save, skips it and preserves the configured startup duration for the next full HA start ([ADR 0043](adr/0043-skip-startup-grace-on-integration-reload.md)).

## Availability issue ownership

Homeostatic disables sibling coalescing with the public library setting `EngineSettings.coalesce_count=None`. Legacy saved `coalesce_count` and `coalesce_window` values remain accepted and round-trip for compatibility, but do not enable sibling grouping. A healthy shared integration does not merge independent availability checks, including failures discovered together after startup or later siblings. Confirmed failed-integration dependencies still suppress and absorb related failures. Recovery retains reconnect grace, then separates remaining failures. Device, entity and policy checks stay independent; notification collection retains each issue's identity and controls. A shared integration issue evaluates each affected monitored availability assignment independently, so integration Morning reporting cannot mask an affected entity's Immediate assignment. Compiler scopes include the selected check identity, so unrelated battery or condition findings do not inherit availability reporting. Public library predecessor records preserve pending deadlines and prior attention through absorption and splitting.

Older grouped issues transition through public split events. `EpisodeOpened.split_from` identifies the predecessor for inherited acknowledgment, pause and attention state; `EpisodeResolved` with `resolution: split` and `split_into` records the exact successor IDs without claiming recovery. The adapter retains these IDs in history and detected events, gives each inherited pause its own cancellable control ID, and withdraws obsolete notifications without an all-clear. Unknown evidence cannot clear previously known failures. Later unrelated failures inherit no controls. The library owns the transition and attention semantics; the adapter never rewrites its snapshots.

A check shared by several recovering integrations has one successor. An existing independent successor retains its controls. A new successor with multiple predecessors has no `split_from` and starts fresh attention; all predecessor histories link to it. This avoids arbitrarily inheriting suppression from one of several issues. Older adapter versions cannot read the separation records; a rollback requires the matching saved-state backup and component and library versions.

## Acknowledgment and early cancellation

Administrator actions `acknowledge(episode_id)` and `cancel_control(control_id)` use public HealthTree APIs under the runtime lock and save before confirming success. Operator actions first drain captured observations and evaluate due monitoring work against the current inventory. They rediscover sources only when inventory has changed or the engine needs initialization; an action alone does not force a full catalog scan. Periodic reconciliation remains unchanged. Acknowledgment uses the authenticated HA context user id; callers cannot supply an actor. The library owns the first time and actor, the cross-recipient effect and restart persistence. An explicit rule option `require_acknowledgment: true` stops acknowledgment-dependent attention only. Recovery, dismissal, shelving and awareness remain distinct. Canceling a control ends only that request; overlapping maintenance and quiet hours remain effective. Missing, expired, removed or replaced control ids are rejected. A storage failure leaves monitoring unavailable and returns an ambiguous result that must be inspected before retrying.

The dashboard exposes these controls only when `inventory.attention_controls_supported` is true, displays acknowledgment from the read-only policy explanation, and disables actions when disconnected or for non-administrators. No notification consumer response is treated as acknowledgment implicitly. Built-in Companion delivery provides an explicit phone Acknowledge action, specified under [Automation-owned alerts and Companion acknowledgment](#automation-owned-alerts-and-companion-acknowledgment); the optional event-consumer blueprint does not. The adapter uses public health-tree APIs for acknowledgment, cancellation and delivery grouping, with no alternate implementation or compatibility fallback.

## Built-in checks

### Repairs

Every active Home Assistant Repair, except those Homeostatic raises, is a `repair` source with its own episode. Its name is the Repair's translated title, its finding names the raising integration and severity, and its link is the automation editor when the Repair carries one and the Repairs page otherwise. The episode ends when Home Assistant deletes the Repair or the owner ignores it. During the startup hold, a stored Repair that its integration hasn't raised again keeps its episode open. Each Repairs policy owns its notification assignments. Up to 500 Repairs are tracked. Trace-only run errors stay outside this contract ([ADR 0044](adr/0044-report-home-assistant-repairs-as-issues.md)).

### Broken automations

Each loaded automation that names an entity id present in neither the entity registry nor the state machine is one `broken_automation` source. The issue uses the automation's name, lists the missing ids and links to that automation in the editor. The check runs after startup, when automations reload, when an entity registry removal or id change asks which automations still name that id, and when a created entity id is named by an open issue; other registry updates do not run it. If the automation component is not loaded, the check does nothing and does not end issues it already opened. The issue ends when the automation no longer names a missing id, the automation is gone or the entity exists again. It is outside the dependency map and does not change enrollment or edit the automation. Enabled Broken automations policies select this check and own their notification entries; turning Repairs off leaves it on. Unavailable, unknown and disabled entities do not open it. Template-only ids, device triggers that never resolved to an entity id, and scripts stay out ([ADR 0050](adr/0050-flag-automations-that-name-missing-entities.md)). The check keeps its fixed scope and explicit missing-entity-reference detection; it is not a general automation debugger.

### Battery maintenance producer

Homeostatic discovers battery percentage (`sensor` or `number` with battery device class), explicit low-battery (`binary_sensor` with battery device class) and charging (`binary_sensor` with battery-charging device class) from HA metadata. Charging alone is context rather than a monitored battery. Battery candidates appear in Monitoring and remain unmonitored until a saved policy selects their `battery` check; each policy enrolls only the check its type names (`availability`, `battery` or `vacuum`). The battery candidate is an edgeless maintenance node, separate from HA availability.

One percentage and one explicit warning on the same HA device are offered as one reviewable battery candidate. When several percentage or warning entities belong to one device, each is offered separately; charging is associated automatically only when there is one battery candidate and one charging entity. An entity without an HA device remains its own candidate. The source uses its primary entity's registry identity where available. A changed source grouping retires the old monitoring identity as removed, never as physical recovery. Source details show all paired entity ids so an owner can leave a mistaken pairing unmonitored.

A current percentage at or below the policy threshold (20% by default) or an explicit low warning is `warn` / `battery_low`; contradictory low and normal reports remain a warning with a conflict marker. A current, confirmed charging report for that battery is `pass` / `charging` even while a low report remains. A current percentage above the policy threshold and an explicit normal warning clear the low condition when all configured low signals are current and normal. Missing, unavailable, invalid or restored low evidence is `unknown` unless another current signal reports low or charging is confirmed. Missing charging evidence cannot itself clear a low condition. If charging stops while low evidence remains, the warning can open again. Battery checks have zero raise and clear hold and no physical-freshness TTL; HA state updates do not establish a device-originated heartbeat. A charging clearance means this monitored condition ended, not that the battery was replaced or is fully charged. Current readings, entity ids and conflicts remain available as evidence; replacement type remains unknown without verified metadata.

### UPS condition producer

Homeostatic maps NUT and APC UPS Daemon entities into one UPS producer ([ADR 0051](adr/0051-monitor-ups-conditions-through-shared-mappings.md)). Discovery uses the integration's full entity unique ids and the device identifier's integration and id, including separate devices on one server; further identifier fields are ignored. A candidate requires mapped raw status and battery charge or remaining runtime. Entity names never identify roles. Disabled diagnostics remain disabled and are shown as optional missing evidence. Conditions begin unmonitored until a named policy selects them.

Each UPS condition is an independent edgeless node: `ups_power`, `ups_reserve`, `ups_battery_replacement` or `ups_fault`. Named policies create stable device/condition identities qualified by policy identity and detection settings. Backup operation warns, replacement warns, and explicit faults fail; NUT bypass warns about lost protection. Mains input alone cannot prove output to connected equipment. No power wiring or equipment dependency is inferred.

NUT reads raw `ups.status` flags. `OB` and `OL` establish backup and mains mode; `LB` and `RB` report reserve and replacement warnings. `OVER`, `OFF`, `FSD` and `ALARM` report faults; `BYPASS` reports lost protection. `TRIM` and `BOOST` alone pass the fault check, and calibration and test flags remain context. APC UPS Daemon maps `ONLINE`, `ONBATT`, `LOWBATT`, `REPLACEBATT`, `OVERLOAD` and `NOBATT`. Its `COMMLOST` report invalidates cached condition measurements rather than asserting a hardware failure. Unrecognized flags are retained in technical evidence.

Reserve can supplement an explicit warning with owner-selected charge or runtime threshold pairs. Values at or below the low threshold warn. Once low, values must reach the higher recovery threshold to clear. Without a custom pair, a readable mapped device low setpoint is used, and recovery requires a value above it. Runtime is normalized from its reported seconds, minutes or hours into minutes. Explicit warnings cannot be canceled by a conflicting numeric value. All enrolled reserve signals must be current and normal for recovery. Charging alone clears none of the UPS conditions. Checks have zero raise hold, the configured clear hold, no physical-freshness TTL and the existing unknown hold.

Missing, unavailable, restored, disabled, invalid or contradictory mode evidence never clears a condition. Retain enrolled signal bindings while their HA device and owning entry exist, including through restart; registry removal of a signal and an orphaned state under its former name are unknown evidence. Explicit device or entry removal ends monitoring as removed. Persist last confirmed power mode, numeric hysteresis and device thresholds independently of the opaque engine snapshot.

Reserve notification rows explicitly select `when: mains` or `when: backup`. Before any confirmed mode, reserve is dashboard-only with a visible evidence gap. Missing mode evidence retains the last confirmed reporting mode while low reserve is established. A confirmed transition into backup mode rearms an earlier acknowledgment through the public `Policy.rearm` API, preserving the issue and its shelf. Returning to mains disables backup-route reminders and leaves the reserve issue open until its own recovery. Changing presentation preserves the existing mains/backup eligibility and renewed-attention behavior.

### Entities not provided producer

The independent `entity_not_provided` check binds a policy to canonical registered entities with an owning config entry ([ADR 0104](adr/0104-entities-not-provided-policy.md)). It includes enabled diagnostic and configuration entities, honors the policy's selectors and explicit inclusion and exclusion overrides, and excludes disabled entities and devices. It never creates a device availability check or changes an availability policy.

After HA has started, a missing entity state or `restored: true` placeholder is a warning observation only while the registered owning entry is enabled and loaded. Missing or mismatched registry ownership, startup and an entry that is not loaded are unknown evidence. A live non-restored entity state passes this condition, including `unavailable`, `unknown` and `off`; availability policies retain their separate state meanings. The check uses the configured `duration_seconds` raise hold (default 86400, integer range 0–31536000), zero clear hold and no TTL. A gap in eligibility interrupts qualification. Unknown cannot clear an open condition and receives record-only attention. Only qualified current warning evidence appears as a current issue or badge; this is the exception to passive availability-gap presentation.

The finding says Entity not provided, identifies the loaded owning integration and names the entity. It does not claim obsolescence, identify an upgrade as the cause or assert a physical fault. The normal issue dialog provides acknowledgment, notification pause and the existing Open Entity action; the owner can inspect the entity through Home Assistant. Devices retain their ordinary issue grouping. No entity deletion or registry-cleanup action is exposed, and Homeostatic does not infer that a registration is obsolete. Removal, exclusion and disablement end monitoring through normal reconciliation; a current live entity clears the condition. Retained records follow the ordinary history contract. Policies owns grouped entity selection, the For at least duration in hours and saved notification assignments.

### Vacuum error producer

Homeostatic offers every `vacuum` entity as its own candidate ([ADR 0046](adr/0046-report-vacuum-error-activity.md)). Candidates stay unmonitored until a saved policy selects the `vacuum` check; an availability policy leaves that check off, and a vacuum policy leaves availability off. A broad vacuum policy may cover current and future vacuums.

The check reads the entity's current activity. `cleaning`, `docked`, `idle`, `paused` and `returning` pass. `error` fails with reason `vacuum_error`. `unavailable`, `unknown`, a missing state, a restored state and any other value are `unknown`, and unknown keeps an open issue open. The check has no raise hold and no clear hold. The finding says Home Assistant reports the vacuum in error. When Home Assistant has an area for that vacuum, the notification names it; that area is the device's assignment in Home Assistant.

The source is its own node and has no dependency edges, so a failed integration does not absorb it. Availability of the same entity, when watched, stays a separate issue. A change from one ordinary activity to another, including `cleaning` to `error`, is observed even though both are available states. Discovering a vacuum sends nothing, and Homeostatic does not command the vacuum.

### Configured temperature producers

Each selected source and named policy has an independent temperature monitor. Temperature limits require a finite minimum, maximum or both, an explicit duration from zero to 365 days, and °C, °F or K. Minimum cannot exceed maximum. Thermostat defaults are a 2 °F permitted margin, 2 °F meaningful progress, 2 °F worsening and a one-hour progress interval; the interval must be positive. Policy copies have independent identities. Detection edits retire the affected check and its pending qualification before starting the replacement; renaming leaves monitoring untouched. The editor for these settings is described under [Policies](#policies) ([ADR 0052](adr/0052-thermostat-setpoint-deviation-alerts.md), [ADR 0053](adr/0053-configurable-temperature-limit-checks.md)).

The sensor producer reads the current state and its `unit_of_measurement`. The thermostat producer reads `current_temperature`, `temperature`, or the `target_temp_low` and `target_temp_high` pair. In the supported HA 2026.9 Core, climate attributes are converted into the installation temperature unit; the caller supplies `hass.config.units.temperature_unit`. The producer does not infer units from the thermostat hardware. These contracts follow the [sensor documentation](https://developers.home-assistant.io/docs/core/entity/sensor/) and [the supported climate implementation](https://github.com/home-assistant/core/blob/2026.9.3/homeassistant/components/climate/__init__.py).

Normalize absolute readings and targets into Celsius and round comparisons to six decimal places. Difference conversion applies the scale only, with no absolute-temperature offset. Equivalent normalized targets do not reset progress. The check observes reported values; repeating a cached value at a deadline does not establish physical freshness. No sensor placement, equipment defect or physical freeze prediction is inferred.

A temperature-limit reading outside either bound starts continuous qualification. Equality is in range. At the duration deadline, an ongoing violation warns; duration zero warns immediately. An in-range reading cancels qualification or clears the condition. A direct low-to-high or high-to-low jump without an observed in-range reading preserves qualification and issue identity, while updating the violated bound. There is no additional recovery threshold.

Thermostat `heat` checks below target minus margin; `cool` checks above target plus margin; `heat_cool` uses its low and high targets respectively. Equality is permitted. HVAC action is context and never a prerequisite or reset trigger. A new target, mode or conditioning direction starts a new comparison context without crediting the smaller target gap as measured progress. Off and unsupported modes return a removal instruction: retire the check as monitoring ended, without issuing a passing observation. Missing or unavailable modes are unknown evidence instead.

Within an unchanged thermostat context, retain the best temperature reached, a last-credited comparison temperature and its time. Improving the best temperature by the configured progress amount credits one new interval from that observation. Smaller steps accumulate; returning to an already reached temperature earns no credit. A fall away from the best temperature by the configured worsening threshold warns immediately. Cooling reverses these comparisons. This best-temperature baseline allows deterioration after useful recovery to qualify. Missing the progress deadline warns as stalled. A usable observation at exact deadline equality is evaluated for recovery and meaningful progress before stall; progress first observed after an elapsed deadline cannot erase the stall. Timers revisit current evidence at the returned deadline.

Once qualified, thermostat progress alone does not clear an issue; entering the margin does. A later worsening observation updates the same issue. A changed target that already satisfies the margin clears with a target-satisfied reason, distinguishing the changed expectation from measured improvement. An open issue remains open when the new target still lies outside the margin.

Missing, unknown, unavailable, restored, nonnumeric, nonfinite, unsupported-unit and malformed target-range evidence emits unknown and discards pending qualification and progress history. It never clears a qualified finding. Current usable evidence starts a fresh observation interval; an already-qualified condition remains qualified until its own recovery evidence or monitoring removal. Persistence retains only the qualified reason. Restart discards pending intervals and temperature comparisons so unobserved downtime cannot qualify a new issue or manufacture progress. The adapter restores the library's issue independently and supplies fresh producer observations. Producer observations have no freshness timeout; check enrollment owns the existing unknown-evidence hold. Availability monitoring remains independent.

Producer evidence includes source identity, normalized temperature, configured bounds or targets, thresholds, current observed interval and an explicit unverified physical-freshness flag. Monitoring and reporting configuration use the validated single-save path; these producers do not request delivery directly.

## Situations and automation alerts

### Dormant functions and situations

Function definitions remain stored but are inactive in production ([ADR 0039](adr/0039-mothball-functions-and-native-options.md)). Their graph nodes, readiness, importance effects, entities, previews and suggestions are not published, and function definitions and external capabilities do not appear in the interface. Internal code remains available for a future design. Existing function entity registry entries are disabled by the integration. New installations have no function setup path.

A situation has its own immutable `id`, name, importance and one bound entity. `on` means active/fail, `off` means clear/pass. Missing, disabled, restored, unavailable, unknown or unexpected states mean unknown, and unknown never clears an open situation. The situation has no dependency edges and is excluded from readiness targets and function requirements. HealthTree owns its episode and policy lifecycle. Situation checks use zero raise/clear holds, an explicit `ttl: null` and the configured unknown hold.

The owner supplies condition logic in Home Assistant. Its template or helper must expose source loss as unavailable rather than off. Setup documentation and inventory expose this unverified availability contract; automatic template inspection is not implemented. Equipment maintenance must never use the global quiet scope or suppress situations; operator controls use only scoped windows.

### Automation-reported situations

A situation declares either an `entity` or a `report_timeout` integer from 60 to 86400 seconds, never both. The latter accepts the administrator action `homeostatic.report_situation` with its configured `situation_id` and `state` (`active`, `clear`, `unknown`). HA automations without a user context use the normal administrator-service convention. Reports never create configuration. Only one automation should own each id; calls are applied in serialized arrival order using adapter UTC acceptance time. Retrying the same state retains the episode and refreshes evidence; it is not a new occurrence ([ADR 0032](adr/0032-automation-reported-situations.md)).

The existing condition check uses report_timeout as its TTL. Reports map to fail, pass or unknown; expiration never resolves an open episode, and the normal unknown hold then governs stale attention. Ordinary reconciliation never renews a report. Every setup or reload requires a fresh report: a restored open episode retains its identity and receives unknown before current reports arrive. The source is edgeless, excluded from readiness and protected from equipment maintenance, like an entity-bound situation.

Actions use the same serialized persistence-before-publication path as operator controls. Storage failure returns an error, publishes no new delivery and marks monitoring unavailable; inspect state after recovery before retrying. No separate report store, lifecycle or notification bypass is introduced. Reports do not enable notifications, create recipients or prove delivery. One-shot informational events are outside this increment.

The supplied automation blueprint checks all declared evidence entities, reports unknown for missing, unknown, unavailable or restored evidence, and otherwise evaluates native HA conditions and reports active or clear. It reevaluates on source changes, HA start, each minute and optional owner triggers; configure the timeout longer than this cadence. Disabling or removing the reporter causes expiry, never clear. Conditions run inside the action sequence so a false result reports clear rather than skipping the automation, and the blueprint checks both the positive condition and its explicit negation so an errored condition (such as a nonnumeric temperature value) reports unknown rather than falling through to clear. Startup races retry on the next minute. Owners must list every entity needed as evidence and use one reporter per id. Conditions describe continuing states; trigger-specific event predicates require an owner-maintained state source instead. HA owns timer and restart semantics.

### Automation-owned alerts and Companion acknowledgment

Automation-defined alerts remain unfinished and are not ready for user setup. The contract below records existing experimental implementation under [ADR 0034](adr/0034-create-alerts-from-ha-automations.md); it does not establish a supported end-to-end product workflow. Remaining setup and notification work belongs in the [roadmap](roadmap.md#other-planned-work).

`report_alert` accepts a saved HA automation entity, optional stable alert_key (default `default`), name, rendered message, profile, active/clear/unknown state and report_timeout (default 300 seconds). First valid reports register a durable source without separate YAML. HA registry unique_id plus alert_key identifies the condition; changes to entity id, alias, text or profile preserve identity, and duplicate automations have different unique_ids. Reports remain administrator services; HA-owned automation calls use HA's existing service authorization convention.

Registration and its observation use the existing serialized snapshot transaction. Invalid inputs mutate nothing. Storage failures publish no new delivery and mark the runtime unavailable until a durable retry. Registration does not enable requests. Outgoing profiles require existing generated reporting settings; missing profile destinations record the issue without fallback and return a visible status. Dashboard only requires no recipient configuration. Monitoring links to the owning automation and shows its profile without a second editable assignment. Metadata updates occur on the next report; report text is limited to 2000 characters and names to 200.

Active registrations are capped at 1000 per installation; retired records do not use that capacity. `manage_alert` explicitly retires or resumes an owner/key. Retirement removes monitoring (not recovery) and blocks refresh from re-enrolling it until resumed. Disabling an automation alone expires evidence to unknown. When HA removes the owning automation from its entity registry, reconciliation deletes its declaration and ends any open issue as removed from monitoring. Optional administrator `adopt_situation_id` transfers an existing report-only declaration to automation ownership while preserving its node and episode; entity-bound declarations must remain entity-bound. The old declaration is retained but shadowed, including while retired, and the old report_situation action rejects transferred identities.

Built-in phone delivery adds Acknowledge to active individual issues, device messages and collected messages. Each opaque callback reference freezes the exact represented episode IDs and is private to the delivery, recipient and phone. It is persisted before transport. A later member or occurrence cannot be acknowledged through that reference. One integration listener accepts only remote events with HA-authenticated, active user context matching the current phone owner and a still-permitted recipient route; payload actor and device values grant no permission. Intended recipients need no admin role for this narrowly scoped callback, while administrator services retain their authorization. References expire after 30 days, are capped at 2000 with oldest eviction and survive restart. Duplicate acknowledgments are idempotent. Callbacks while delivery is disabled or after a route is removed do nothing.

Phone dismissal and clear requests are not acknowledgment or receipt. Main taps follow [Notification tap destinations](#notification-tap-destinations). Non-device summaries, digests and resolution notices have no acknowledgment button. Legacy event-only consumers retain their existing behavior; authenticated phone actions are provided by the built-in person/phone sender. Evidence alone resolves conditions. Tests use isolated HA and mocked phone transports.

## Operator controls

`homeostatic.shelve` holds new notifications for one current episode until an explicit time. It applies to every recipient, including urgent notifications, reminders and escalations; it does not acknowledge or resolve the problem. Existing messages remain visible, and the library may update them silently or clear them on resolution. Pending library deliveries remain subject to their other holds. A shelf can be extended or ended early with the explicit cancel-control action. Situation episodes can be explicitly shelved by their own episode id.

`homeostatic.start_maintenance` creates an engine quiet window for one equipment capability (`entity`, `integration` or `external`), optionally including its dependency-graph dependents. No global scope is exposed. `preview_maintenance` returns the current scope and already-open episode ids without changing anything; the start action recomputes and returns the scope applied. Dependents follow the graph as it changes during the window. Situations have no edges to or from equipment; both graph validation and maintenance scope validation enforce their separation, and situation roots or any scope containing a situation are rejected.

Maintenance prevents new episodes in scope. It keeps observations, readiness, existing episodes and their notifications active. A continuing fault opens after expiry subject to the library's normal rules; a fault that recovers inside the window produces no later episode. Shelving an existing episode is a separate explicit action.

Maintenance creation is available only through the published Home Assistant actions. The panel has no entry point, duration and scope form, preview or submission path for it, because its preventive use case does not justify another household workflow ([ADR 0087](adr/0087-remove-maintenance-creation-from-ui.md)). Existing and action-created windows remain visible with their target, expiry, optional reason and End maintenance control; removing the creation UI does not cancel saved windows.

Both mutations require HA administrator access when called with a user context; HA-owned automations without a user context follow HA's standard admin-service authorization. Both accept `until` as an ISO timestamp with a timezone, normalized to UTC, strictly in the future and at most seven days away. There is no default expiry. Optional `reason` is at most 500 characters. Actions serialize with observation processing, reconcile captured evidence first, and persist before returning success or publishing resulting notification requests. Requests already authorized in the durable outbox retain their replay identity; controls do not retract previously authorized events. A storage failure returns an error and makes monitoring unavailable until persistence recovers; an errored request may remain in memory or on disk, so inspect controls after recovery before retrying.

`operator_controls` and inventory list active requested controls with a generated id, target, start and expiry, actor user id (null for system context), reason and dependent-scope flag. This presentation is persisted separately from opaque library snapshots. Expired controls, shelves for resolved episodes and maintenance for removed nodes are removed. Deadline scheduling includes control expiry, even when no notification is due. Reload restores the library's holds and the adapter's control records together. These are current controls rather than a permanent audit history.

The dashboard offers the pause (a shelf) from an open issue. Its form requires an explicit local end date and time, converted to zoned UTC, and allows an optional reason of at most 500 characters. It discloses target and duration before submission, explains that the pause holds new notifications for every recipient, including urgent notifications, without resolving the problem or removing existing messages, and saves directly after validating target and expiry. Afterwards it shows the confirmed applied scope and expiry. Live updates preserve form values. A disconnected, unavailable or non-admin view cannot submit; a resolved shelf target or ended control cannot submit. Duplicate clicks while a request is pending cannot send duplicate actions. There is no automatic retry after an ambiguous action failure: the form asks the owner to inspect active controls after recovery. Successful actions show the returned saved control, expiry and reason. Active controls are readable in issue and source details, each stated once with target and expiry; eligible problems offer Acknowledge issue, and current temporary controls offer Resume notifications or End maintenance without nested panels. Unsupported actions are absent; temporarily unavailable actions include a reason. Controls and history use existing schema-version-1 fields and native service responses without adding another runtime update path.

## Notifications and attention

Notifications default to off. The integration emits `homeostatic_notification` events for policy deliveries. Selected `phone:<HA device registry id>` and `notify:<HA entity registry id>` channels also request delivery through Home Assistant notification actions. Other channel ids remain consumer-owned; the optional automation blueprint continues to use the event. Persistent notifications are reserved for failures of Homeostatic itself. Built-in routes send requests directly and optional consumer automations may handle events; both paths are requests rather than proof of delivery.

Named monitoring policies own their notification entries. Current notification definitions compile those entries into the library's recipients, schedules and ordered rules. Compilation preserves independent notification-entry identities, explicit dashboard-only choices and restart state. Saved raw attention-policy data is not an alternate configuration path. Each entry compiles to an opaque health-tree attention route and its own report group. Routes evaluate independently and keep their clocks even when requests selecting the same saved notification share collection. Acknowledgment is issue-scoped and stops acknowledgment-dependent reminders; scheduled summaries continue to include eligible open issues, including acknowledged ones. Reports omit resolved problems and empty messages. Temperature unknown-evidence findings do not send another availability warning. Grouping follows policy authorization and does not change the library's scheduling decisions.

On activation, the integration asks the library to restart attention for current open episodes after reconciling observations. Episode history and age matches remain unchanged. Escalation starts at activation; reminders start with the first delivery request. Reusable notifications enter their saved collection window after eligibility. Supported inline Custom findings retain their existing device and activation-summary presentation. Urgent and delayed recipients can receive messages at different times. Digest-only problems wait for their named report, and record-only problems produce no event. An empty activation produces no problem notification. Ordinary reload restores clocks without replaying openings. Reusable notification edits restart only changed routes; name-only edits retain clocks and delivered state. Built-in phone routes process withdrawal requests even when outgoing requests are off, using the previous tag; optional event consumers receive the same withdrawal event. Enabling requests can authorize messages for already-open episodes after reload.

A Home Assistant start holds notification requests until watched integrations finish loading, for at least startup_grace and at most startup_quiet_max. Reusable collection windows begin when that startup hold ends. Supported inline Custom findings retain their existing device or per-route startup presentation. An overdue reminder updates its subject's existing message once; restart does not replay openings or accumulated reminders. Scheduled reports keep their report contract. Reloading the integration while HA runs has no hold. See [events.md](events.md#restart-and-activation).

### Collection

After attention authorization, public health-tree `DeliveryCollections` collects reusable notification requests by saved notification identity, recipient, resolved destination and compatible accepted instructions. A common device, integration or cause is unnecessary. Different saved identities stay separate even with identical names and settings. Inline Custom assignments retain their existing compatible device presentation. Independent issues retain their evidence, clocks and controls. Public immutable membership records supply exact issue and route identities; persistence snapshots remain opaque. Reusable collection keys identify the saved notification, compatible accepted instructions, recipient, resolved destination and window. Scheduled report groups preserve entry identity; supported inline Custom device messages retain their subject identity.

The first eligible request starts a fixed collection window. Later requests never extend its deadline. `timing.collection_delay_seconds` is a required whole number at least zero for named immediate, acknowledgment and daily-hours notifications; zero releases without waiting. These routes override ordinary policy batching with zero to avoid stacked waits. Startup holds precede the collection window. Overnight daily-hours requests enter only when hours open; ended issues stay out. A deadline after hours close waits for the next allowed opening without a second collection delay. Scheduled reports bypass collection entirely. Empty windows send nothing. Newly eligible requests after closure start another window; silent updates retain their original message membership ([ADR 0102](adr/0102-collect-requests-by-saved-notification.md)).

The library persists pending membership, deadlines, accepted instructions and resolved targets together with delivery state. Edits to saved definitions do not retarget pending requests or restart their delay. Removed assignments and ineligible issues leave pending membership. Absorption preserves pending and presented attention without replaying an opening; new assignments remain independently eligible. Each outbound collection lists its exact issue IDs, and acknowledgment applies only to IDs shown in that request. Partial recovery updates the remaining members without clearing them. Transport replay retains the durable delivery ID and accepted channels.

A supported inline Custom device message keeps its tag across new findings, partial recovery and restart. Partial recovery silently replaces it with the remaining authorized findings; only the last member ending withdraws it. Monitoring removal is distinct from observed recovery. A changed device association moves the affected finding to its current notification subject on the next authorized message update, without claiming recovery or withdrawing other members. Registry changes alone do not create attention. A new qualifying issue can request attention even if earlier members were acknowledged. Presence is resolved for new requests before grouping. Silent updates and withdrawal retain prior destinations without adding unseen findings to destinations that did not receive them.

Events distinguish open, activate, update, remind, escalate, resolve, summary and digest actions. Identical reminders are still emitted; silent identical replacements are deduplicated. Scheduled digests retain their report grouping and route identity. Supported inline Custom startup and activation findings for devices and standalone entities use their continuing subject messages; summaries for other source kinds retain their existing per-recipient and route contract. A summary resolution silently refreshes the remaining membership, and its last resolution clears its tag. A later individual reminder or escalation can move an issue out of a non-device summary. See [events.md](events.md).

`policy` returns public library explanations for current episodes. `preview_policy` validates an unsaved policy and restores an isolated copy through the opaque public snapshot API, then simulates activation at the current time. It returns initial delivery requests, evaluated rules, recipients, pending times and the next deadline, without saving or sending, including while notifications are off. It is an activation preview rather than a forecast of future evidence. The public attention preview accepts an explicit simulation policy without saving it. ## Persistence

Persist engine state, policy state, episode presentation, authorized delivery-group membership, last requested messages, delivery attempts and a delivery outbox in a versioned HA Store. Restore policy before feeding engine restore events. Reconcile current observations before releasing pending openings; cancel an unsent opening when its episode has resolved. Save before firing events and save the cleared outbox afterwards. A crash between emission and acknowledgement can replay the same delivery id: delivery is at least once. The built-in sender saves one attempt per delivery id and channel before calling the HA service, so a plain message is not repeated after replay; this can lose a message if the process stops after the attempt is saved and before the service call. Phone requests use a stable tag for replacement. At the built-in phone sender and Companion blueprint boundaries, tags exceeding 64 UTF-8 bytes become the lowercase SHA-256 hexadecimal digest of the complete tag; shorter tags remain unchanged. Opening, updating and clearing use the same transformation, preserving recipient separation and Apple APNs collapse-id limits. Public event tags and persisted delivery identity remain unchanged. HA mobile-app services can log provider errors without raising them, so the absence of a caught service error does not prove provider acceptance. Event consumers must replace by tag or deduplicate by delivery id. Event publication and a successful service call are not acknowledgements of receipt.

Use atomic Store writes and read-back verification because Store can log a write failure without raising. Verification compares the stored file with the bytes the Store serializer produces for the same envelope, and parses and compares the content only when those bytes differ, so a serializer change cannot by itself make monitoring unavailable. Construct the snapshot under the runtime lock and keep it unchanged until verification finishes. Configure Store to serialize in the executor; verification also runs in the executor so large snapshots do not block the HA event loop. A refresh that queued no notification request and changed no episode, operator control or setting defers its save to a Home Assistant timer of at most 15 seconds, and later such refreshes inside that window add no save; a request, an episode or control change, a settings save or shutdown saves immediately and verified, which also flushes a pending save ([ADR 0096](adr/0096-coalesce-evidence-saves-and-verify-bytes.md)). The snapshot persists durable facts: issue identities and history, operator controls, the delivery ledger, enrollment, monitoring identities and scopes, and timing progress. Route signatures persist as digests of their canonical JSON. One evidence map holds the latest observation of every source for the detail views and persists, as the `evidence` section, only the last known failure and whether history is complete; a failure that continues with the same reason keeps its first report, a source with no known failure and complete history is not persisted, snapshots with the earlier `check_evidence` and `integration_evidence` sections still load, and the first refresh after a restart rebuilds the current observation of every source. A save whose serialized bytes equal the last verified write performs no write; health-tree 0.9.0 keeps its engine and policy snapshots free of volatile observation state so that an unchanged refresh produces equal bytes ([ADR 0097](adr/0097-shrink-the-snapshot-and-save-durable-changes.md)). A settings save whose write or verification fails answers the caller with an error, marks monitoring unavailable and reloads the integration from the last verified store while the config entry keeps its previous options; there is no in-process rollback ([ADR 0100](adr/0100-a-failed-settings-save-reloads-from-the-store.md)). Evidence arriving during persistence remains queued for the next refresh. Invalid snapshots remain visible failures and must not silently discard history. Native invalid-JSON handling remains with HA and its storage-corruption Repair. Unsupported development envelopes fail visibly; the current envelope retains issue identities and delivery records across reloads.

The storage envelope is version 3 and requires validated `monitoring_identities` bindings and `monitoring_scopes`, which records the previously evaluated exclusions for each policy. Device bindings use the policy, canonical source and detector settings without exclusions as a stable lookup key. A version-2 envelope seeds its actual legacy check IDs from the saved policies before restoring the opaque engine snapshot. Version 3 never treats missing or invalid continuity metadata as an old installation. Current policy configuration remains authoritative; the stored scope is used only to distinguish a saved exclusion edit from registry changes. Preview computes the same proposed identities without updating the bindings or scope records. Removing the integration cleans up owned delivery requests from either supported envelope version and deletes history with the rest of its owned storage. This storage change requires paired state and component rollback for any subsequent installation.

Unload and shutdown cancel all callbacks, drain captured observations and attempt a final save. A failed final save leaves the last durable snapshot and creates a visible Homeostatic storage error; cleanup still completes so there is no stopped runtime registered as a failed unload. A platform refusal to unload keeps monitoring running. Work already saving cannot create a new timer after shutdown starts.

## Detected facts

Homeostatic publishes `homeostatic_episode` and `homeostatic_control` events for every opened, meaningfully updated and resolved episode and every operator control start and end, independent of notification settings ([ADR 0014](adr/0014-publish-detected-facts-for-owner-automations.md)). Facts publish after the durable save and before notification requests from the same change, at most once, without replay on reload. Readiness sensors are enum sensors. Function event entities are dormant. [events.md](events.md#detected-facts-for-owner-automations) is the payload contract.

## Issue history

Only a library `EpisodeResolved` event creates a history entry. Unknown evidence, shelving, maintenance and notification disablement are not resolutions. `cleared` records the library's recovery decision; `removed` records the end of monitoring without observed recovery; `absorbed` links to the absorbing episode, which may remain open or later fall outside retention; `split` links to continuing independent issues without claiming recovery. Resolution time is when the adapter handles the event, so after downtime it does not claim when the physical problem ended. A problem that clears before any notification was requested still appears in history.

The read-only `resolved_history` action and additive `inventory.resolved_history` field return `{started_at, retention, episodes}`. `retention` contains `max_episodes: 100` and `max_age_days: 30`. Entries are newest resolution first. The adapter keeps only the latest 100 terminal episodes whose resolution was observed less than 30 days ago across the installation; expiry is applied to queries immediately and to persistence at reconciliation. This is bounded terminal history rather than a full transition, configuration, operator-action or delivery journal; a settings and notification audit journal is separate scope.

Each entry contains `episode` (the public serialized library Episode, retaining its stable id, original opening time, findings and display labels), `resolved_at` (UTC ISO), `resolution` (`cleared`, `removed`, `absorbed` or `split`), `absorbed_into` (episode id or null), `split_into` (successor IDs, empty for other outcomes) and `source` (a display snapshot or null if the source is unavailable). The source records `node_id`, `name` and `kind`, plus known `owner_id`, `monitoring_source` and integration and device attributes for historical association. Runtime removal captures the previous source before replacing the inventory, so a resolved child remains discoverable from its recorded parent after rename, registry removal or reload. Existing entries without ownership metadata remain valid and retain limited association; current topology is not used to invent their historical membership. Episode findings and status describe the last episode evidence rather than a current health claim. Source display text can fall back to the episode's labels when no current source exists. A rename or later fault does not rewrite an earlier history entry.

History is saved in the same atomic, verified envelope as engine and policy state and the outbox. Restore loads it before handling engine restore events. Duplicate episode ids are not appended on replay. Malformed stored history fails setup visibly and leaves the stored envelope intact. Older envelopes with no history field start an empty history at their first running evaluation; `started_at` identifies when collection began, without reconstructing earlier resolutions. Read responses are detached, produce no notifications and do not advance the engine or policy. Existing `inventory.episodes` remains the open-episode list.

### Selectors and source selection

Monitoring type determines the available selectors and parameters. Supported selectors include all eligible sources, All except selected, selected sources, areas and labels; values within an area or label selection match any selected value. Integration sources lack area and label attributes, so those selectors are not offered for them. Matching groups include future eligible sources. Selected integrations use integration domain/family identity, displayed with the integration name, and include every current and future configured connection of that family. Account and phone names may supply context but are not separate integration choices. Existing connection-specific selections must not silently broaden to a family during reading or saving unrelated edits. Repairs always covers all Repairs and Broken automations always covers all automations. Temperature policies select climate entities or temperature-class sensors. Availability group counts exclude disabled sources and diagnostic or configuration entities, while exact entity choices retain their supported opt-in behavior.

Entity availability and Entities not provided select entities through one tree, described under [Policies](#policies). Save policy applies the complete scoped draft, including an empty selection. Existing positive selectors and type unions and complements retain future matching; `included_sources` and `excluded_sources` provide explicit per-entity overrides. Exact exclusion wins over inclusion, which wins over a whole-device exclusion and the dynamic base scope. Whole-device exclusions are retained for migrated saved policies and cover future members. An exclusion affects only its policy; independently configured checks retain their scope.

Entity inclusion and exclusion edits retain unaffected check identities and attention. Changes to detection parameters or positive selection replace the applicable expectations. Published schema-two detector aliases are retained for supported entity conditions. No device-member identity or scope bookkeeping remains. Removing an expectation ends it as monitoring removed, never observed recovery. Unsupported thermostat modes remove the applicable expectation; later supported evidence re-enrolls it.

### Named notifications

`notification_definitions.profiles` maps stable profile IDs to `{name, timing, methods}`. The six supplied choices are Immediate with a 60-second collection delay; Immediate with acknowledgment with a 5-second collection delay, repeated every 30 minutes; Urgent with a 60-second collection delay, daily from 08:00 inclusive to 22:00 exclusive; Morning summary at 08:00 daily; Evening summary at 18:00 daily; and Weekly summary on Sunday at 08:00. Scheduled summaries have no additional collection delay. They use the configured Home Assistant IANA time zone and remain ordinary editable definitions with a stable identity. Add, edit, duplicate and delete use the same lifecycle for supplied and custom definitions, and future updates preserve edits and deletions. Empty methods are valid, visibly unconfigured and produce no requests.

Timing supports `mode: immediate`; `mode: acknowledge` with a positive `remind_every`; `mode: window` with distinct `start` and `end` HH:MM times; or `mode: report` with one `at` HH:MM time and nonempty distinct `weekdays`, Monday 0 through Sunday 6. Immediate delivery includes overnight. A daily window uses its saved collection delay during open hours and holds outside them until the next opening; a problem that ends before then sends no delayed notification. Window-specific recipients have stable distinct aliases while retaining delivery-method channels; existing non-window recipient identities remain unchanged. Summaries include eligible open problems, including acknowledged ones, and omit ended problems and empty messages.

Policy notification rows store a stable `id` and `profile`, with `when: mains` or `when: backup` for UPS reserve. Existing inline `{id, timing, methods, when?}` rows remain supported without rewriting; their methods list is nonempty. Reading, validation and restart perform no automatic conversion, and these rows appear as Custom notification with their effective timing and methods. Choosing a named notification explicitly replaces the row with `{id, profile, when?}`, preserving its identity and UPS condition. Each row retains independent reporting. Renaming a profile or policy preserves references. Rejection of earlier pre-policy test schemas is unchanged.

A policy can assign several named notifications, while zero assignments means dashboard-only monitoring: issues still appear in Open issues and monitoring continues. Each policy owns its assignments; the chosen reusable notification owns timing and selected methods. Editing a shared notification affects its saved uses, and changing a policy assignment affects only that policy.

### Delivery methods

`notification_definitions.methods` holds reusable delivery methods ([ADR 0056](adr/0056-ha-delivery-methods-and-presence.md)). A method contains selected Home Assistant destinations and optional presence selection; fixed methods may combine phone, notify-entity, Assist announcement, TTS and configured SMS action targets. Optional integrations are not runtime package dependencies. Presence selectors save the chosen `person.*` entity IDs; the catalog exposes `entity_id` separately from internal person and user identities. Presence selection retains home/away destinations or selected people's destinations with an explicit fallback or Skip choice. Current presence is resolved for a new request or test; merely arriving home sends nothing, and a current destination preview does not guarantee a later destination.

Generic notification entities and actions have no inferred email or SMS capability. Only a verified SMS provider offers recipient-number fields; an unclassified notification route remains generic. Available targets must meet the transport's capability requirements. Unavailable saved targets remain visible for repair; new-selection lists hide unavailable targets by default.

Phone destination choices group a Companion phone route and its mobile-app notify entity by their shared HA device registry identity, never by display name. Each device appears once per person. Existing selected route ids remain unchanged on viewing; a selected notify-entity route remains selected and usable. A new choice prefers the Companion phone route. Clearing a phone removes all selected aliases for that device in the draft; selecting it again chooses one route. Distinct device registrations remain separate even when their names match. Other notify entities remain independent destinations. These presentation rules edit no HA registrations and send no messages.

Deleting a used profile or method requires replacement or explicit removal of its references in the same save and blocks relevant pending drafts; deletion cannot leave dangling references. Replacing a profile preserves assignment IDs and UPS conditions. Removing its assignments leaves other policy rows intact. Removing a profile's last method keeps the profile and its assignments visibly unconfigured; removing the last method from a supported inline row removes that row. Any combined operation names its complete scope and applies atomically without saving unrelated drafts. Pending edits to affected definitions or policies must be resolved first.

### Saving configuration

`configuration` returns current editable settings, supplied policy templates and selection metadata; it accepts `section: settings` or `section: delivery`, and omitting the section retains the combined response for existing clients. Policy and settings reads use the running adapter's validated settings and do not rediscover delivery capabilities. Saved-settings responses run directly in the WebSocket handler without a second queued task. Reads that include settings wait for an in-progress save to complete when a runtime is available, then return the matching settings and revision from the current runtime; reads while the runtime is absent return not-ready. Uncontended settings reads remain synchronous. A response captured before a later save retains its older revision and is rejected if submitted after the saved configuration changes. Delivery discovery begins when a method editor opens and never replaces the configuration draft; discovery failures show an explicit retry while retaining edits.

Administrator-only `preview_settings` and `save_settings` accept the supported settings object and saved revision. Preview changes no live state or delivery. Save validates the exact submitted payload, checks the saved revision, serializes with other saves, rejects stale revisions, verifies the new durable state before publishing success and restores previous options if applying the draft fails. Scoped frontend drafts submit only their intended changes over the saved configuration; each policy, reusable notification, delivery method and installation settings editor saves only its scope and leaves unrelated drafts intact. There is no separate Review button, review screen or preview prerequisite; any internal preview or token exchange runs within the scoped save action, and public read-only preview APIs remain available. Reading, editing and internal validation send nothing. Global notification activation saves independently of editor drafts. A confirmed policy save finishes the action; there is no Undo control or reversal record. Current consumer-automation choices remain saved unless explicitly changed.

Ordinary monitoring-policy edits apply inside the running monitor under its state lock. They drain previously captured observations, preserve unaffected issues and reporting clocks, and verify the new durable state before publishing success. Notification-only edits reuse unchanged source inventory; changes to other policy fields or pending inventory changes still trigger source reconciliation. Monitoring and reporting evaluation run on every save, and observations arriving during storage remain queued. Static catalog presentation reuses serialized source and candidate rows when their records are unchanged; changed records regenerate only their own rows, and an enrollment rule change regenerates only the candidate rows whose decisions changed. A policy name edit relabels its existing sources and public engine declarations without rediscovering source membership, leaving effective notification instructions and queued request identities unchanged. Detection and enrollment edits still reconcile the applicable expectations. Selective restoration adds explicitly chosen missing supplied policies to the draft without overwriting surviving policies.

Changes confined to `notification_definitions.profiles`, `notification_definitions.methods` and their corresponding generated policy apply in place under [ADR 0067](adr/0067-save-delivery-methods-in-place.md). This includes method name, destination and presence edits, creation and deletion, with reference validation and explicit replacement or removal of uses. Accompanying policy edits may change notification assignments only: policy identities, order and all other fields must remain unchanged. Combined definition and source or detection edits, policy additions or deletions, other policy-field changes, time zone, legacy default/Repair/automation assignment definitions, global sending and installation timings use the full reload path. Eligible definition saves keep the same runtime, registered panel and subscriptions active. A settings save that requires reload retains panel registration through apply and rollback, publishes monitoring as unavailable during the transition and reconnects the existing editor to the resulting runtime; ordinary unloading and removal still remove the panel. Enabling notifications or changing an active consumer requires the selected automation to be enabled.

Effective route signatures compare saved instructions against one captured source inventory and restart only affected reporting. Names and unchanged instructions preserve acknowledgment, clocks, delivered state and pending delivery identities. Effective destination or presence-rule changes clean up prior messages using their original resolved targets. Accepted reusable collections retain their instructions, targets and deadline while their assignments remain eligible; other obsolete pending requests are cancelled. Queued deliveries never resolve presence again; new requests follow the current saved method and current presence evidence. Timing and destination changes apply to open issues on save: superseded requests are withdrawn where supported and changed reporting activates. Immediate reporting can notify again, reports wait for their next occurrence, and name-only changes do not reactivate reporting. While global sending is off, new issue notifications stay suppressed and silent cleanup of prior messages retains its delivery contract. Changing notification settings does not prove delivery.

Confirmed availability saves compare JSON settings by value, ignoring object-field order while preserving array order and all revision, draft and scope safeguards. [Persistence](#persistence) defines what happens when the write or its verification fails.

### Release reset of earlier configuration

Setup replaces the otherwise-valid earlier named-notification configuration whose non-report profiles all lack `collection_delay_seconds` with `initial_settings` in the installation timezone. The maintainer authorized this release reset instead of conversion: it replaces configuration data, clears saved options and starts with notifications off, no delivery methods and the current supplied policies and notifications. Compatible runtime history remains in the store and follows normal restore and reconciliation. Recognition accepts the published device-policy shape for this reset without restoring retired device checks. Mixed old and new profiles or other invalid settings fail visibly without reset. Current-format settings, including edited policies, explicit dashboard-only choices, Custom assignments and deletions, remain unchanged on ordinary saves, reloads and restarts; interface changes must preserve supported saved configuration and runtime state. Earlier pre-policy schemas remain unsupported, and earlier test configuration models have no compatibility editor or migration requirement; the installation guide describes that testing transition and backup choices.

## Interface

The integration registers a Homeostatic sidebar dashboard at `/homeostatic`, a reusable `custom:homeostatic-card` and a `custom:homeostatic` dashboard strategy. The strategy creates the five primary destinations: Open issues, Monitoring, Policies, Notifications and History, with Open issues as the opening page. Settings is a labelled header utility for installation timing. There is no separate Overview destination; existing Overview and functions links open Open issues, other older card view names remain accepted, and existing user dashboards are not rewritten. The dashboard baseline follows [ADR 0019](adr/0019-accepted-dashboard-baseline.md) and the later decisions cited below. Frontend updates version the panel, the generated-card element names and every module URL together, so loading a new module in a retained Home Assistant session registers a new current element instead of selecting an older constructor the browser already holds; previously configured card names remain accepted, and an already-open app must still refresh after installation.

The dashboard is administrator-only because the aggregate view includes installation-wide inventory, configuration and notification routing. Read and configuration WebSocket commands enforce administrator access; hiding the sidebar is not the authorization boundary. Operator forms call the existing HA administrator services, which enforce authorization independently. Non-administrators get an explicit access message. Existing readiness entities remain available through HA's entity access controls. Frontend content treats names, findings and reasons as text, including when creating links to native HA configuration.

### Purpose before controls

First identify the user's task and the page or dialog that owns it, using the [page responsibilities](#navigation-and-page-responsibilities). Reuse that workflow from other contexts instead of building a second place to perform it. Shared workflows retain their action semantics while adapting controls to the context, and show selection controls only when there is a real choice. Then, for every heading, label, count, control, section and navigation step, ask: why is it here? What does it tell the user? How does it help them make a decision or take an action? What would they lose if it were removed? Name the concrete benefit. Available data, a reusable component or a familiar design pattern is insufficient reason to show something. Use grouping to establish relationships before adding labels to individual rows. Remove elements that add no useful information or capability; keep necessary evidence, action scope and state visible. Internal grouping labels and counts do not earn space merely because the system tracks them. Explain an action's complete effect where the action is offered.

Do not add expandable sections by default. A disclosure needs substantive supporting content and a reason that hiding it helps the current task. A refresh timestamp alone, repeated evidence or the same buttons do not justify Technical details or another layer. An allowance for at most one Details section is a limit rather than a requirement. Keep short useful information inline and omit information without a purpose. Inventory branches and large affected lists can justify expansion when it makes the complete set manageable.

Before adding a detail link or chevron, compare the starting view with its destination. The destination must add useful evidence or open the workflow that owns the next task. A Home Assistant link or contextual coverage action can stay beside the source it serves; issue response opens the shared issue dialog. Preserve separate issue identities and complete action scopes while removing repeated presentation. Apply this test to prototypes, implementation and review.

Expandable lists share left-hand chevrons, keyboard focus styling and child indentation throughout Monitoring, policy selection, History and issue groups: a minimum 44-pixel target, 24-pixel indentation for nested source branches, and expanded history details aligned with their row label. Branches start collapsed unless navigation or search reveals a specific item. Selecting a row and expanding its children are distinct actions, and expanding never selects, saves or changes monitoring.

### Controls and visual standards

Use native or accessible Home Assistant controls with visible labels and correct roles, Home Assistant's theme colors, font family and component conventions, and the shared tokens below rather than one-off values in each page. Review both light and dark themes.

| Control | Use | State and label |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Switch | An immediately applied binary operational setting, such as global notification sending | Stable subject label, explicit On/Off text and a position reflecting confirmed state. Pending action is separately labelled. |
| Checkbox | A draft boolean field, optional feature or explicit selection for an action | The label describes the selected option. Save applies a draft field; a named action applies the selected scope. Use an indeterminate state only for an actual partially selected group. |
| Radio group | One choice among a small visible set | Label the group and each alternative; show the saved or draft context. |
| Select or searchable picker | Longer supported choices, including source and destination selection | Preserve selections through search; show the selected set and any unavailable saved choices. |
| Action button | A command, such as Save policy, Acknowledge issue or Send test | Name the result or operation. Its fill never indicates current enabled state. |
| Link | Navigation to an issue, source, editor, history record or Home Assistant page | Name the destination. Give meaningful destinations real routes and link semantics. A policy name that opens its editor is a link rather than a badge-shaped button. |
| Disclosure | Substantive optional content that passes the purpose test | Its label describes the useful hidden content and the task it supports. Required names, conditions and actions remain visible. Opening it reveals its contents without nested disclosures and does not select, save or change monitoring. |
| Contextual help | A brief explanation of a field or choice, with optional setup links | A small help icon beside the label opens a compact popover that overlays the form without moving fields or changing the draft. |
| Tabs or segmented navigation | Sibling views with distinct useful content | Show selection separately from keyboard focus; preserve the selected object. Do not create an empty tab just to redirect elsewhere. |

A switch keeps a stable accessible name such as Notifications while its state text changes between On and Off; it never alternates between Turn on and Turn off ([W3C switch pattern](https://www.w3.org/WAI/ARIA/apg/patterns/switch/)), and policy status is shown explicitly regardless of control styling. Switches for immediate settings and checkboxes for draft fields or explicit action selection is a Homeostatic design convention. Blue does not mean Off. A filled Turn on button describes an available action and must never be the only indication of present state; where immediate toggling is supported, the switch pattern replaces alternating Turn on and Turn off status buttons. Color never carries status by itself, and acknowledged, paused, cleared, unavailable and unmonitored remain textually distinct. There is no green general-purpose Healthy badge for a loaded integration or an empty issue list.

Contextual help stays brief and on demand. Its icon has a descriptive accessible name and a usable touch target. The popover opens by click, touch or keyboard, supports keyboard access to its links, and closes on Escape, outside interaction or a second activation of the icon; Escape returns focus to the icon without closing the containing editor. It stays within the viewport on narrow screens and never becomes an expanding Help section that pushes the form down. Required instructions, validation and warnings stay visible beside their fields.

| Role | Visual treatment | Meaning |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Primary action | Filled theme accent with readable text | The main next action in this task, such as Save policy. |
| Secondary action | Neutral outlined or text control | Another available action, such as Discard changes or Edit policy. Outlined dialog buttons and links share the same theme accent color. |
| Destructive action | Explicit label and danger treatment where appropriate | An action such as Delete policy with consequences described in context. |
| Switch On | Accent track, consistent thumb position and On text | Confirmed enabled state. |
| Switch Off | Neutral track, opposite thumb position and Off text | Confirmed disabled state. Off is not itself an error. |
| Selected navigation | Subtle accent surface or indicator, neutral text and semantic selection | The current destination or item. Primary navigation combines a theme-accent indicator and selected surface with its accessible current-page state. |
| Focus | Distinct visible outline | Keyboard focus; it remains distinguishable from selected state. |
| Warning | Warning surface or icon with specific neutral text | A condition requiring attention or a material evidence limitation. Open issue indicators use labeled warning-tinted surfaces with neutral text. |
| Error | Error surface or icon with specific neutral text | A failed operation or established problem condition, according to its meaning. |
| Success | Success surface or icon with neutral text | A confirmed successful operation or a specifically established state. |
| Neutral status | Readable neutral text | Not monitored, dashboard-only, disabled by choice or other ordinary context. Disabled entries use neutral styling and do not imply intentional disablement. |

Type roles are page title, section heading, item title, body and supporting text, starting at 24, 18, 16, 14 and 12 CSS pixels in scalable units, with weight 400 for body and 500 for emphasis; pages do not invent intermediate sizes. Essential conditions, action labels and validation use body size or larger. Supporting text carries secondary context and is never the sole location for a warning or required instruction. Editable fields on narrow touch layouts use at least 16 CSS pixels. Use tabular figures for aligned counts and times.

Spacing uses a 4-pixel basis with 4, 8, 12, 16, 24 and 32 pixels: smaller gaps within a control or row, larger gaps between tasks, and related content visibly closer than unrelated sections. Use theme surfaces and shared border-radius roles and reserve elevation for overlays. Avoid cards nested inside cards and large empty bordered panels around a few lines of content. Source, issue and action dialogs share 24-pixel desktop and 16-pixel narrow insets, 16-pixel section gaps and a visible header while scrolling. Keep page content, headings, field labels and row text left-aligned, with consistent row anatomy: name and useful context, current condition or saved state, then one way to open the item and applicable actions; full findings, usage lists and technical explanations belong in details. Prefer quiet separators over repeated boxes. Keep primary actions visible and put infrequent actions such as Duplicate and Delete in a labelled More actions menu that opens a compact popup sized to its contents and bounded by the viewport, supports keyboard access, closes with Escape or an outside click and changes no draft settings. Ordinary views omit raw node IDs and empty technical disclosures.

Target WCAG 2.2 AA. Switches support Space and expose their state. Navigation, trees, disclosures and dialogs support keyboard operation without requiring hover. Dialogs manage focus, close predictably and restore focus to the initiating control. Mark selected tabs and expanded branches semantically. Normal text needs at least 4.5:1 contrast and qualifying large text at least 3:1; required control boundaries, meaningful icons and state indicators need the applicable non-text contrast, verified in actual light and dark theme combinations ([text contrast](https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Understanding/contrast-minimum.html), [non-text contrast](https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Understanding/non-text-contrast.html)). Primary touch interactions use 44 by 44 CSS pixel effective targets as the product target; WCAG's AA minimum is 24 by 24 CSS pixels with specified exceptions, and dense desktop layouts still need adequate target size and separation ([target size](https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Understanding/target-size-minimum.html)). Review ordinary content at 320 CSS pixels wide and at enlarged text or zoom without loss of information or required horizontal scrolling, reflowing lists and forms while preserving reading order ([reflow](https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Understanding/reflow.html)). Use polite status announcements for saves and state changes; reserve alerts for actionable errors and avoid announcing every live update.

### Editors, drafts and saving

Each editor names its object and save scope: Save policy, Save notification, Save delivery method or Save settings. Monitoring settings and notification assignments belong to one policy editor and draft. Rendering changes no configuration, and navigation retains the complete pending draft. A read-only panel and an unchanged editor have no save footer, disabled Save, Cancel or No changes message; navigation remains available independently of editing actions. Cancel is reserved for abandoning a bounded dialog or nested edit whose scope is explicit.

| Editor state | Actions and feedback |
| --- | --- |
| Unchanged | Show saved values and contextual navigation; no dirty-state controls. |
| Changed | Show Unsaved changes beside the editing task, Discard changes and the scoped Save action. |
| Saving | Identify the submitted scope, preserve its draft, and disable editing and repeated submission while the request is pending. Show Saving, or Applying changes when the installation change requires a runtime reload. |
| Saved | Notification and delivery-method editors return to their matching list and focus the saved object's Edit action when the same editor is still active, monitoring is connected and no newer edits remain. Other editors remove dirty controls and retain contextual return. No persistent saved message or success banner. Newer edits or navigation during saving prevent an automatic return. |
| Edited again after saving | Restore dirty controls and clear the previous save confirmation. |
| Discarded | Restore an existing object's saved baseline in place and remove dirty controls. For a new object, abandon the unsaved draft and return to its origin without leaving an empty saved object. Earlier completed saves remain saved. |
| Failed, uncertain or conflicted | Retain the draft, name the error or what became stale, and keep correction or reconciliation available without overwriting newer saved changes silently. |

Field labels stay visible above nearby controls; placeholders only supplement them. Pair fields only where they express one choice. Provide required indicators, units and supported ranges next to inputs. Put validation beside the field and an error summary where useful; after submission move focus to the relevant error and keep the draft intact. Save validates and applies the submitted draft once, with concurrency and rollback protection behind that action and no compulsory review screen. When saved settings are reconciled, preserve any draft state newer than the submitted values and keep it visibly unsaved. If applying succeeds but refreshing fails, say that settings were saved and current data could not be refreshed. A network timeout after a mutation may be ambiguous; inspect current state before repeating the action. Typing and monitoring updates preserve the draft, focused field and cursor position, and focus changes caused by redrawing a form do not trigger another redraw.

While its own settings request is pending, an editor keeps its draft and remains visible through a runtime reload. Temporary runtime unavailability during that owned transaction does not replace the editor with the general outage screen. A confirmed save awaiting the first current runtime update may keep the editor read-only with Finishing update. Failure, an actual runtime error after confirmation, or changing the editor or Settings section ends this exception; a disconnect or failed save stays explicit, preserves pending edits and cannot imply success. Disconnection prevents saving until fresh evidence and configuration are available.

Shared editors make their save scope explicit and show Used by before applying a change: a reusable notification lists affected policies, and a delivery method lists its notification and supported inline uses. Opening a shared notification or delivery method from another editor retains the originating draft and a contextual return; saving the shared definition changes its saved uses without applying a pending policy assignment or another editor's draft, and Save policy applies only the policy. Concurrent edits surface a conflict instead of silently overwriting one another. Delete identifies the object and its consequences and follows the reference rules under [Delivery methods](#delivery-methods); Cancel leaves definitions and assignments intact. Ordinary saves and reversible operational changes do not require a generic confirmation sequence.

The global Notifications switch is a persistent header control available throughout the interface, with the explicit state On or Off and the explanation Monitoring continues when notifications are off. It reflects confirmed runtime state rather than an unsaved editor field, changes only saved global sending, and preserves all editor drafts without applying them, creating delivery methods or discarding edits. On permits requests according to policy configuration and current eligibility; it does not promise an immediate message. Turning it off removes no policy entries and stops no monitoring. Pending or error feedback sits beside or directly below the control; confirmed success appears in the saved state and an accessible announcement, without a routine banner about existing issues each time sending is enabled. A saved global notifications-off state is also explained beside a policy's assigned notifications.

| Switch state | Presentation and behavior |
| --- | --- |
| Confirmed On or Off | Show the matching switch position and text. Name the scope, such as All Homeostatic issue notifications. |
| Action pending | Keep the last confirmed state identified, show Turning on or Turning off, and prevent duplicate submission. |
| Confirmed success | Update the switch and state from the accepted response, announced accessibly without moving focus. |
| Known failure | Retain the last confirmed state, explain the failure and offer an applicable retry. |
| Result uncertain | Say the outcome is not confirmed; refresh the saved state before permitting a blind repeat. |
| Disconnected or unavailable | Label any displayed value Last known and make unsafe mutations unavailable with a reason. |

In a policy editor, Enabled belongs to the draft and takes effect on Save policy. Immediate policy-list switches were not part of the reviewed workflow; if introduced, they need a defined saved-state scope and handling for a pending draft of the same policy, and Continue editing instead of a competing quick toggle is a proposed pattern. Saved-state quick controls require authorization, conflict protection and a confirmed result through the normal configuration owner.

### Identity, counts and terminology

Names describe real sources. Use available friendly names and location to disambiguate; keep raw identifiers in secondary or technical detail unless no useful name exists. Within a device, shorten a repeated prefix only when the remainder still identifies the entity, and keep a full entity name when it matches the device name. Do not fabricate a room, name or relationship. Use canonical source names without policy suffixes; policy names identify contributions. Use Entity availability for the entity availability policy in examples; actual saved policy names remain user-defined.

List labels and counts must help identify a problem, assess its evidence or choose an action. Counts name their unit and scope: investigation groups, unique issues, devices, distinct entities or checks. Deduplicate real entity identity before displaying an entity total. Headline scope counts distinct devices and entities without a device separately and never adds device summaries and their member entities into one source total. An investigation group can contain several issues, one engine issue can contain several recorded findings, and independent policy checks retain their own identities; label those measures separately and make each advertised set inspectable. An issue shown in several contexts contributes once to the global issue total. Entity totals are separate from issue totals, and an entity count never substitutes for an issue count or evidence of another condition. Omit internal grouping labels, Shared issue labels and repeated cross-device counts when they add no decision value. Missing identity or type metadata stays explicit and never becomes an invented device or entity count.

| Avoid as a standalone message | Prefer |
| --- | --- |
| Related sources need attention | The affected names and their specific reported conditions, grouped under a device where known. |
| Separate check | Checked by [policy name], with the check type and its purpose. |
| Included with device | Used by this device's availability check, with the responsible policy. |
| Not selected | Not included in this check; show any other checks separately. |
| Problem actions | Direct actions such as View issue, Acknowledge issue or Pause notifications. |
| Change monitoring leading to a generic policy list | Edit policy beside its name, or a choice among the applicable policies. |
| Delivered | Notification requested, Request failed or another outcome supported by the provider evidence. |

Use notification consistently for outgoing messages and their controls. ### Live updates and subscriptions

Cards share one subscription per HA connection while mounted and release it when the last card is removed; cards replaced within the same JavaScript turn retain their shared subscription and current view, and final cleanup runs at the next microtask when no card remains. Dashboard navigation does not request monitoring evaluation: a new subscription receives the adapter's already-completed snapshot immediately. A full browser reload opens a new subscription and briefly says Loading dashboard while receiving that snapshot, without claiming monitoring has never completed. A connection-ready event cannot replace a snapshot or error already received after reconnection. Replacing the HA connection or changing the signed-in user or administrator permissions clears card editing state, and obsolete pending responses cannot overwrite or block the new configuration. Startup, storage error and unload publish `available: false`. Subscriptions survive entry reload and end on client unsubscribe or disconnect.

Without `compact: true` or `paged: true`, `homeostatic/subscribe` sends a schema-version-1 snapshot after subscription acknowledgement and on runtime publication. It contains availability, current equipment readiness, inventory, active episodes, operator controls, coverage, policy explanations and HA location names. It never reads engine snapshot internals or advances time.

The optional `compact: true` subscription uses schema version 2 ([ADR 0010](adr/0010-large-inventory-updates.md)). Its first available event includes the complete catalog, areas and floors with `inventory_changed: true` and a `catalog_revision`; subsequent events at that revision omit those static fields and set `inventory_changed: false`. Dynamic problem, readiness, coverage, history and control fields remain complete replacements. A catalog change, reconnection, runtime replacement or recovery from unavailability sends a full baseline, and clients reject a delta without a matching available baseline. Disconnect invalidates the compact baseline; only a new full baseline restores current status. Compact source tables render at most 50 rows per page and search the complete received inventory by name, identity, kind or monitoring label; search and page survive evidence updates, a location change resets the source selection, and cached client lookup and location models are replaced with the catalog baseline.

The shipped frontend requests `paged: true, panel: true` (schema 3). It receives current evidence and source context for open episodes and active controls without the full discovered catalog. The response has `panel_context: true`, omits installation-wide `coverage`, and includes recorded and reason members, canonical sources, owning devices and integrations and their locations. `inventory.monitored_count` counts the complete watched scope so empty Issues states remain accurate before catalog loading. Subscribers without `panel: true` keep their full, compact or ordinary paged contracts. `catalog_revision` and `catalog_sections` identify a coherent set of static lists; views that need the complete inventory load it on demand with `homeostatic/catalog` (revision, section, offset and limit from 1 to 200, default 200). Each response contains the same revision, section, offset, total, items and next offset, or null when complete. Stale revisions return `stale_catalog`, and unavailable monitoring returns `not_ready`. The endpoint is administrator-only and read-only ([ADR 0029](adr/0029-load-catalog-pages-on-demand.md)).

The catalog revision changes only when section content changed, compared by row identity: `node_id` for nodes and candidates, `id` for areas, devices and floors, the value for readiness targets, and appended records for enrollment changes. An inventory signal starts rediscovery but advances the revision only when source metadata, candidates, targets, enrollment history or browsing locations actually change; area, floor and device browsing-name changes still advance it even when monitored sources are unchanged, and unchanged rediscovery keeps the loaded Monitoring tree without repeating the download. When the change fits in one page of rows, the schema-3 event announcing the new revision also carries `catalog_delta`: the previous revision and, per section, the inserted or replaced rows and removed keys ([ADR 0095](adr/0095-patch-catalog-after-edits.md)). A client whose complete catalog is loaded at that previous revision patches it in place and requests no page; any other client, a larger change, or a mismatch between the patched sections and `catalog_sections` reloads the pages.

The Issues list renders from the panel context without requesting the full catalog. Opening an availability issue requests the complete catalog needed to review all contributing availability policies; its Stop Monitoring control waits for that catalog and shows a retryable loading error on failure. Other issue details resolve canonical source coverage through the bounded source endpoint. One shared client fetches at most four pages concurrently when an inventory-dependent view needs it and installs the completed catalog atomically, merging the latest dynamic evidence. Partial pages never imply a complete search or an empty healthy inventory. The first Monitoring visit waits for a complete catalog. Once loaded, a new revision without an applicable delta keeps the previous complete tree available with an updating notice while replacement pages arrive; the notice identifies results as potentially out of date and offers retry if loading fails, fresh issue-context rows overlay the previous catalog, and no partial replacement catalog is shown. Unavailability, disconnect and final unsubscribe discard in-flight results. Revision changes coalesce into one replacement fetch, and errors require explicit retry. Once the replacement finishes, search covers every source in the current revision. Settings and Notifications render from the administrator configuration response, which supplies policy area and floor names, without waiting for the source catalog. Source-tree structure and picker indexes reuse unchanged catalog inputs while dynamic evidence stays current. Source and issue details allow one read in flight and coalesce subsequent refresh requests; selection, disconnection and authentication changes invalidate obsolete replies.

### Other WebSocket commands

All dashboard WebSocket commands enforce administrator access. Subscription, node detail, configuration reads and previews do not mutate state, acknowledge problems, start maintenance, shelve or send notifications; operator services and explicit saved-method tests retain their separate contracts.

`homeostatic/node` accepts one `node_id` and returns its source, public explanation, potential impact and readiness (null for situations). Unknown nodes return `not_found`; unavailable monitoring returns `not_ready`. Source replies include paged current entity readings, complete counts, total and next_offset; a device lists its eligible operational entities with unavailable and absent readings first, and the reply lists the saved policies contributing actual entity and condition checks. Entity and condition node details use their own captured evidence.

`homeostatic/preview_delivery_method` accepts `{method_id, revision}` for a saved reusable delivery method and returns `{method_id, name, targets, presence, skipped, preview_token}`. Each target contains `{channel, name, type, available}`; presence maps each selected person entity to `{state, restored}`, with null state for missing evidence. Preview reads current evidence without advancing attention, changing configuration, writing the delivery outbox or making any transport request. Unsaved or missing methods and stale configuration revisions return `invalid_method`; unavailable monitoring returns `not_ready`.

`homeostatic/test_delivery_method` accepts `{method_id, revision, preview_token}`. It recomputes the preview and requires its token to match the saved revision, method definition, current presence evidence and resolved target list including availability. Changed evidence or destinations return `preview_required`; an unavailable selected target returns `invalid_route` before any request. The method editor runs this resolution and validation within its single Send test action. An explicit test requests a fixed message for exactly the resolved configured targets, independently of outgoing issue notifications, and does not create an episode, change attention clocks or enable notifications. The response is `{requested: [channel], failed: [{channel, error}], skipped: boolean}`; a provider failure does not turn into presence fallback or imply failure of another target, and an explicit skip selection sends nothing. Test requests do not use the issue outbox, and success reports a request rather than receipt ([ADR 0059](adr/0059-save-directly-and-test-configured-methods.md)).

`homeostatic/save_settings` validates and saves the current policy configuration as described under [Saving configuration](#saving-configuration); `configuration`, `preview_settings`, `policy`, `preview_policy` and `resolved_history` are described in their owning sections. Other read-only queries and actions expose inventory, explain, readiness, impact, coverage and rollup through public library APIs. An overall readiness sensor preserves `ready`, `unknown`, `degraded` and `blocked` for watched equipment, and no selections yield unknown. Diagnostic sensors show open episode and evidence-gap counts; evidence-gap diagnostics count watched checks rather than the unmonitored catalog records kept for browsing. The raw coverage query retains the library no-checks list. Episode presentation is persisted independently of engine snapshot internals.

Document the retained pattern's variants, states, labels, keyboard behavior, narrow layout and confirmation of saved state. Review a switch, a policy row, an issue summary, an affected-entity list, a source detail, a method editor and a history row together so their conventions agree. Validate each against the completion criteria above, including unavailable data, pending operations, failed saves, shared-method changes and unsaved navigation, using the same criteria for automated scenarios, isolated browser walkthroughs and authorized pilot review.

### Settled questions and remaining limits

Primary navigation, investigation grouping, canonical source presentation, scoped dirty-only editors, reusable named notifications and contextual method editing are settled; planning should not reopen them. Of the page structures considered, Issues first was selected through the October 2026 walkthroughs because it starts with problems and keeps configuration directly reachable. An operational home would have required combining the issue workspace into Home or maintaining competing starting pages; a Configure area would have added a layer between source investigation, policy edits and notification configuration; and Sources first would still have needed a global queue for cross-source issues, Repairs and automations. The walkthroughs do not establish measured usability or performance across real installations.

| Design detail | Required boundary |
| --- | --- |
| Ordering, pagination and very large catalogs | Preserve exact totals, complete reachability and selection. Measure representative data before choosing thresholds. |
| Group labels for less common source types | Preserve known relationships and provide an independent-problem fallback. No invented parent failure. |
| Data for affected lists, history and notification decisions | Verify recorded membership and evaluated decisions are exposed. Missing data stays explicit; backend additions need behavior coverage. |
| Shared-definition deletion | Preserve reference integrity and explicit scope. Offer replacement or removal and block relevant pending drafts. Validate atomic application and concurrent changes through the runtime save path. |
| Optional policy-list quick controls | The reviewed Enabled checkbox saves with the policy draft. A list switch and its handling of a pending draft of that same policy remain proposals; independent global sending is already accepted. |
| Initial setup, Settings and active-control layouts | Apply shared standards and existing capability boundaries. Maintenance creation stays outside the panel. |
| Refresh, restart and concurrent drafts | Preserve drafts and surface conflicts within the supported session. Restart recovery of unsaved edits requires separately verified storage. |
| Final type scale, density and responsive details | Use shared tokens and verify keyboard, touch, zoom and both themes. |

No engine correlation change, new monitoring type, broad migration or reset, live test, release or deployment follows from a design approval on its own.

## Acceptance scenarios

Tests use isolated Home Assistant instances and explicit time changes, with mocked delivery actions and no live notifications. Current policy scenarios cover operational eligibility and exact diagnostic selection, registry disablement and re-enablement, missing exclusions and future enrollment, per-policy device-member evidence and queued observations, independent findings and holds, device grouping, frozen acknowledgment membership, presence changes, route removal, replay, restart and guarded saves. Configuration acceptance covers integration-family grouping with multiple connections, future matching devices and explicit policy selections, separate mobile navigation, full-inventory search, live-update scroll retention, Open issues and Monitoring, timing edits across sections, exact-preview validation, stale saves, invalid settings, preservation of unrelated options and reload rollback. Policy lifecycle acceptance covers immutable type, supported selectors, independent detection and notifications, duplication, deletion, selective restoration, shared methods, acknowledgment, scheduled summaries, exact-payload saves and restart persistence. The temperature timeline fixture covers duration equality, gaps, restart, conversion, oscillation, changed targets and progress. Existing source, dashboard and automation-reporting scenarios remain required. Isolated validation does not establish provider delivery or browser and device usability; release validation uses the published health-tree pin, and live acceptance remains a release gate.

## Development dependency

The integration manifest, project dependency and lockfile pin the published health-tree 0.10.0 package. It provides `DeliveryCollections`, `CollectionRequest`, `Policy.delivery_eligibility`, `Notification.replaces` and `Rule.batch`, the public independent attention routes, delivery grouping, and the collection and predecessor contracts this specification relies on. Release validation installs that published package without an editable sibling checkout. Cross-repository development can use the documented editable override until a library release is published.

Home Assistant installs the manifest requirement through its normal dependency mechanism. The reproducible manual-install archive includes dashboard assets and build identity without a sibling library checkout. Home Assistant 2026.9.3 is the tested baseline. ## HA registry references

The integration uses the public automation-reference helpers in the [pinned HA automation component](https://github.com/home-assistant/core/blob/2026.9.3/homeassistant/components/automation/__init__.py) for suggestions, and Home Assistant's [entity registry](https://developers.home-assistant.io/docs/entity_registry_index/) and [device registry](https://developers.home-assistant.io/docs/device_registry_index/) interfaces for identity and metadata. A registry device belongs to the single config entry Home Assistant records for it; discovery and phone-route lookup read that owning entry directly and never the removed multi-entry set, which Home Assistant 2026.10 reports as deprecated on every read. A device's integration comes from its eligible member entities when they have one and otherwise from the device's own owning entry. Runtime contracts are tested against the pinned Home Assistant version above; registry associations do not imply physical-device health.
